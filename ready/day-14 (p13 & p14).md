"If deviant and sinful people see us as a disturbance, that is good. We will definitely disturb maya if we have any potency. If we are not disturbing maya, it means that we do not have that much potency."

## Slide 5A: The Living Example — Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura (Part 1)
### **Flattering the Public vs. Speaking Truth**

> **Please Janārdana, Not the Janatā:**  
> Well-wishers often suggested that he soften his uncompromising stance against pseudo-devotional groups and worldly philosophies to maintain public favor. He cautioned his disciples:  
> *"A flatterer can neither be a guru nor a preacher... Don't speak to please the janatā (people in general), but to please Janārdana (Kṛṣṇa)... The public may not like our message, yet it is incumbent upon us to state the truth. It is our responsibility to strongly refute ideologies opposed to Vaiṣṇava dharma."*

> **Failing to Disturb Mankind:**  
> *"Everyone is eager for adoration by others, not for the absolute truth. Those who make a show of being preachers do not disturb mankind, but rather maintain everyone's present mentality while busily protecting their own existence. Therefore there is no propagation of the truth, since one's popularity is not served by speaking or hearing the truth."*

*Source: Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Vaibhava, Vol. 2*

---

## Slide 5B: The Living Example — Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura (Part 2)
### **Causing a "Disturbance" vs. Spiritual Potency**

> **The Incident at the Public Meeting:**  
> Disciples were worried after their bold preaching against impersonalism caused a public uproar, fearing their spiritual master would reprimand them for causing trouble:  
> *"Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta's disciples had been very outspoken at a public meeting and had denounced a highly regarded Māyāvādī monk... The remark had caused a disruption at the meeting, and some of the disciples reported the incident to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta, thinking he would be displeased that his disciple had caused a disturbance.*  
>  
> ***But Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta was pleased and remarked: 'He has done well.'*** *His displeasure occurred, rather, when he heard of someone's compromise."*

> **Speak the Plain Truth:**  
> *"Whenever Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta wrote or spoke the Vaiṣṇava philosophy, he was uncompromising... 'Why should you go flatter?' he would say. 'You should speak the plain truth, without any flattery. Money will come anyway.'"*

*Source: Śrīla Prabhupāda-līlāmṛta, Vol. 1*

## Slide 6: The Root of the Problem (Principle 14)
### **The Blind Spot: Māyā Strikes Our Weakest Point**

"Each person has his or her own unique weaknesses. Maya knows our greatest weaknesses, and it is amazing how she works. Whenever we try to become more serious, maya will attack more than ever."

"Just as in boxing or in any other sport, the opponent will take advantage of a person’s weak spot and strike in that area. Maya has a duty to see if we are ready to go back to Krishna, and by testing our weaknesses, she will determine our readiness."

"If we are completely oblivious, then a little push completely knocks us over."

---

## Slide 7: Principle 14 — Desiring the Auspicious
### **The Core of Surrender: Accepting and Rejecting**

Principle 14: "Whatever is auspicious should be desired."

"For some people, their greatest weakness could be their attachment to relatives who are not devotees. On their birthday, such a devotee might think, 'If I stayed at home, my family would sing happy birthday, bring a cake with candles, give me nice presents, and tell me how wonderful I am. But now I’m on the street selling books and most people don’t even know it’s my birthday.' For such a devotee who has a problem with this issue, meditating on their family could ultimately distract them from the path of devotion."

---

## Slide 8: The Subtle Attacks of Māyā

"Maya might test another devotee by tempting her with wealth. Perhaps her grandmother dies and she gets a letter, informing her that the grandmother left her an estate. She was just a humble devotee with two saris, serving in the temple, but now she has all of this wealth. First she begins to think of all the ways it can be used in Krishna’s service, but after seeing the estate, her mind begins to contemplate all the new possible ways to enjoy while observing all the available material amenities. Gradually, maya might take the person away from devotional service. For this reason, the Bhagavad-gita explains how the mind can be the greatest friend or the greatest enemy."

---

## Slide 9: The Strategy of the Spiritual Warrior
### **Honest Self-Assessment & Fortifying Vulnerabilities**

"However, if we know our weaknesses, we can protect ourselves by putting some extra attention in that area. We can reinforce those areas because we realize where the attacks will come."

"In some cases, we may have to avoid certain environments and associates from the past, especially in the neophyte stage."

"If we always consider that maya is just around the corner, constantly waiting for a chance to attack as soon as our guards are down, we will be vigilant and maya will not cause as much trouble. If we know someone is going to hit us, we can withstand the attack by bracing ourselves."

## Slide 10: The Living Example — The Test of Inheritance
### **Śrīla Prabhupāda on Wealth, Security & Full Surrender**

> **The Trap of Material Security:**  
> In Washington, the temple president recently received an inheritance. Apparently his idea was to use it to provide security for his wife and family, freeing himself for preaching. Prabhupāda didn’t think much of the idea:  
>  
> *"I think Bṛṣākapi should follow the example of Rūpa Gosvāmī. Rūpa Gosvāmī took sannyāsa and gave 50% in charity, 25% for family use, and he kept 25% for emergency. Kṛṣṇa wants to see that the life is sacrificed. But also accumulation, money, should be given to Kṛṣṇa. **Life to Kṛṣṇa, and money to wife, is not a good decision.**"*

> **Forty Rupees Added with Kṛṣṇa:**  
> He offered himself as an example:  
> *"Just see practically. Our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement was started with forty rupees. Now that forty rupees added with Kṛṣṇa, it has become forty crores. You see practically. When I started for your country, I came to Māyāpur. I offered my obeisances to my spiritual master. Then I went. At that time I had no money even to purchase the ticket. And after that, I have come with forty crores. This is the secret.* **Ye yathā māṁ prapadyante.** *If you fully surrender to Kṛṣṇa, then Kṛṣṇa is there."*

*Source: A Transcendental Diary, Vol. 1 by Hari-śauri Dāsa*
