# Day 16 Slide Deck: Guru Is Not a Flatterer (Principle 18)

## Slide 1: Title Slide
### **Sacred Community Course**

**Day 16: Guru Is Not a Flatterer (Principle 18)**
Systematic Study of Principle 18: Chastisement, receiving honest feedback, and overcoming the defensiveness of the false ego.

**Speaker:** HH Bhakti Dhira Damodara Swami Maharaja
**Course Text:** *Reflections on Sacred Teachings V* (Principle 18)

---

## Slide 2: Principle 18 — Guru Is Not a Flatterer
### **The Heavy Duty of the Preacher**

“Someone who is just trying to ingratiate or flatter others can never be a good preacher because he or she won’t address the issues or speak straightforwardly. Such people can never become gurus because a guru is supposed to be heavy. The guru’s main duty is to chastise the disciples. Being in the material world means that we are deviant.”

---

## Slide 3: The Surgeon and the Boil
### **Curing the Disease of Deviation**

“It is the guru’s duty to chastise us, and between chastisements, he gives us some encouragement to prepare us for the next chastisement. Therefore, the spiritual master cannot be a flatterer. If you are sick but the doctor tells you that you are healthy, you will not get the necessary medicine and treatment. Srila Prabhupada gives the example of a person who has a boil. The boil cannot be cured simply by blowing air on it; rather, the patient needs proper medical attention.”

---

## Slide 4: Loving Attack on the Mayavadis
### **Addressing Spiritual Suicide**

“I once met a group of Mayavadis who practically exemplified Srila Prabhupada’s description of them. They approached me once, asking, ‘Why is your Guruji always attacking us? Why does he have to attack?’ I told them, ‘It is because he loves you. He is trying to help you see that, by thinking that the Supreme Personality of Godhead does not have relationships, does not have a body, or does not give and receive love, you are an offender. He wants to help you stop committing spiritual suicide.’”

---

## Slide 5: The Image-Conscious Disciple
### **The Mirage of Sentimental Shelter**

“When one type of disciple comes to see the spiritual master, he or she gives an update, asks for mercy, and then leaves. Basically, such disciples never ask for any advice, nor are they ready to hear any instruction. They simply want a guru who will connect them to the parampara so that they themselves feel bona fide. They never write or ask the spiritual master, ‘What is your desire? What would you like me to do? What do you see that I need to work on? How can I be a better devotee?’ Some devotees have this type of relationship with their spiritual master. They just inform him of their own ideas; however, such devotees will never make any serious advancement.”

---

## Slide 6: The Argumentative Disciple
### **The Cheated Cheaters**

“Another type of disciple will ask the guru questions, but as soon as the guru gives an instruction, they have ten reasons why another solution would be better. They do not make too much advancement either. They can go on for many years chanting Hare Krishna, but they will not advance much because they haven’t really entered into the consciousness of bhakti. They are still on the religious platform, almost like the karma-kandis, although they are involved in the general activities associated with transcendence.”

---

## Slide 7: Ready for Chastisement
### **The Sincere Student’s Privilege**

“However, the disciple who wants to know what the guru wants is a disciple who the guru can chastise because he or she is ready to make the most advancement. If the spiritual master is not chastising a disciple, he might feel that the student is too stubborn or unable to handle it. If the spiritual master is chastising a disciple, it means that he is instructing the devotee with the desire to push him or her forward faster. For a disciple who is serious, the spiritual master can be very direct and scrutinize the situation to ultimately help the person make advancement at a faster pace.”

---

## Slide 8: Asking Peers for Feedback
### **The Wisdom of Servitor Cooperation**

“Therefore, a proper disciple not only asks the spiritual master for guidance, but he or she also asks other devotees for insight on how to improve. We can ask our associates who have served with us over a period of years for advice and feedback. Who knows us better than somebody who has served with us for five, ten, or fifteen years? Not only do they know our bad habits and superficialities, they also know our good qualities.”

---

## Slide 9: Defending the False Ego
### **The Illusion of Personal Image**

“When you do ask another person for feedback or criticism, take a piece of paper and write down what they say. Then reflect on their feedback later. It is helpful to write it down because we have the tendency to defend ourselves when another person starts speaking honestly. This is the nature of the false ego. In order to approach devotees in this way, we have to have some honesty and some real teamship. Otherwise, devotees have the tendency to not be completely honest with each other.”

---

## Slide 10: Bhakti Tirtha Swami’s Traveling Austerity
### **Writing Down the Criticism**

“During that time, I was on traveling sankirtana and would regularly ask the other devotees in the group to point out any areas that I needed to work on in my devotional life. Or I would ask them to just share anything about myself that bothered them. And sometimes they would give a long list: ‘You chant too loud. You are always reading while everybody is chanting. You are puffed up because you want everyone to see that you have finished your rounds already. Why don’t you honor the feasts? At least you should enjoy Sunday feast. You should not be artificially austere.’”

---

## Slide 11: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection (Integration Bridge)**

1. The Boil and the Blow: Bhakti Tirtha Swami explains that the guru’s duty is to heavy-handedly chastise our deviance, and a doctor who simply blows air on a boil cannot cure it. Why is the false ego so resistant to correction, and how can we transition from wanting sentimental flattery to eagerly welcoming spiritual chastisement?

2. Reciprocal Peer Feedback: Sincere devotees ask their long-term service associates for honest criticism and write it down to bypass the mind’s natural defensiveness. How can we build enough trust and teamship in our local communities to give and receive this kind of ego-shattering, non-diplomatic feedback?

---

## Slide 12: Hrdaya Seva
### **An Immediate Heart Offering**

**The Austerity of Sincerity (60 Seconds):**
Close your eyes and sit in quiet stillness. Bring to your mind a Godbrother, Godsister, or spiritual guide who has recently corrected you or whose critical feedback you have been avoiding. Honestly look at the knee-jerk reaction of your false ego to defend itself. Mentally drop those defenses, and make a conscious, quiet vow today to invite mature correction into your life.

**The Action Vow of Sincerity:**
Take a blank slip of paper and a pen from your desk. Write: *“Please write down any areas where my pride, loud speech, or behaviors are agitating the devotees. I promise to reflect on them with sincerity.”* Hand or send this private request to a trusted, mature peer or mentor today, praying to Lord Caitanya: *“My dear Lord, please cleanse me of deceit and keep me forever in the position of a simple, corrected servant.”*
