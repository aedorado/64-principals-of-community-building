## Slide 2: Principle 19 — Auspiciousness of Non-Deceit
### **Facing Reality Honestly**

Principle 19: "It is better to remain in millions of species like birds, beasts, insects, and worms than to become deceitful. One who is free from deceit receives auspiciousness."

"Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains the degraded condition of one who is deceitful. It is better to not even pretend to be a human being because a person who is too duplicitous is not in human consciousness. Human life begins at the mode of goodness, which means that the majority of people walking the planet are not even humans."

"Human life means that one must honestly face the facts of birth, death, old age, and disease. By understanding the miseries of life, one will not lead a deceitful life of just living to ensure one’s own eating, sleeping, mating, and defending."

---

## Slide 3: Principle 20 — Simplicity Is Vaiṣṇavism
### **Straightforward Faith vs. Complex Struggle**

Principle 20: "Vaiṣṇavism is the other name of simplicity. The servants of the paramahaṁsa Vaiṣṇava are simple; therefore, they are the best brāhmaṇas."

"Simple does not mean foolish or naïve. There is a difference between the two. Krishna consciousness is very, very difficult for the complex, bewildered, and faithless person, but it is very simple for the straightforward and faithful devotee."

"Some people come to the movement and last for a year or two. Later they again come and go, continuing the cycle for ten or fifteen years. There are other people who get a book, hear the holy name, and come to visit the temple just once, but remain fixed and never leave. Such a devotee does not even consider going back into maya or deviating. They have full conviction because they are not duplicitous. They are simple."

## Slide 5: The Gravity of the Holy Dhāma
### **Amplification of Consciousness**

"Spiritual tourists move from temple to temple and might even travel to the holy places of pilgrimage, but they do not make any advancement. Unfortunately, sometimes the opposite happens. India is very powerful, but it is also a very dangerous place. The holy tirthas are dangerous because whatever a person does gets amplified."

"When spiritual tourists visit these places, they go in the wrong mindset. Instead of benefiting from the environment, they actually become offenders due to improper association and thinking. But we must go in the right consciousness; otherwise, there will be consequences. We go and beg for Krishna’s mercy. We take the dust from the environments and place it on our head because it is a benediction."

## Slide 6: The Living Example — The Steps in Los Angeles
### **Rushing In to Make Offenses**

> “Once when I was in Los Angeles, Srila Prabhupada was in his quarters and the devotees were rushing upstairs to see him, but they were practically knocking each other over on the way.  
> 
> There was one really eccentric devotee who would sometimes make some very sharp statements. As I was walking up the steps, I noticed him just standing there, mumbling something to himself. I listened closer and heard him say:  
> 
> *‘I don’t know why they are all rushing so fast to see the spiritual master because they are just rushing in to make offenses.’*  
> 
> I thought about his observation and realized that he was right. Some of the devotees did not fully understand the spiritual master; consequently they came in the improper consciousness... when we approach holy people or places, if we have an improper consciousness, we will make *dhāma-aparādha* and in this case *guru-aparādha*.”
> 
> *— HH Bhakti Tirtha Swami (Reflections on Sacred Teachings V)*

## Slide 7: The Living Example — Śrīla Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī (Part I)
### **The Stench of False Prestige vs. Ordinary Stool**

> **Rejecting Deceptive Visitors & "Blessing" Hunters:**  
> When Girish Babu offered to build a small cottage so Bābājī Mahārāja would not suffer in the rain and heat, Śrīla Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī replied:  
> *“I have no anxiety residing in my small hut. I have only one trouble. Many persons come to me and very deceptively ask again and again, ‘Kṛpā koro! Kṛpā koro! Please give us your mercy!’ They are not interested in their own genuine benefit, and as a result of their coming my bhajan is disturbed. If both of you could just give me some of your stool at this place where I perform my bhajan, that would be favorable. Then I would be able to chant ‘hare kṛṣṇa’ day and night. By depositing your stool, people won’t like to come here.”*  
> 
> When Girish Babu's wife hesitated, Bābājī Mahārāja added:  
> *“I am not a vaiṣṇava. Those owners of large temples who wear long matted hair are actually vaiṣṇavas. I have not gotten any result in the performance of my bhajan. Therefore, the place where I reside is fit to receive your stool.”*  
> 
> *Translated by Vyenkata Das Brahmachari from Amara Prabhura-kathä, a collection of articles written by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati* Prabhupada.
> 
> **Sitting Near the Municipal Lavatory to Avoid Duplicity:**  
> Bābājī Mahārāja never entered the room Girish Babu built, residing for six months where people passed stool:  
> *“For him the smell of false prestige and the desire for women and wealth was more obnoxious than the smell of ordinary stool... One may question, ‘Why was your Guru Maharaja sitting on the ground in a municipal lavatory?’ Because he did not want anyone to disturb him. So many people would come to him for darśana and āśīrvāda [blessings], and he did not like it... [He thought:] ‘They will not take any spiritual instruction. They are thinking that, "Here is a saintly person, and he’ll bless me that my one thousand rupees will become ten thousand."’ Gour Kishore Das Babaji Maharaja did not like this botheration. He was sitting by the side of a municipal lavatory so that, ‘These rascals will not come here because of the bad smell. They will not disturb me.’*”
> 
> - HDG SP Lecture in Mayapur. 6 October 1974.

## Slide 7B: The Living Example — Śrīla Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī (Part II)
### **The Chaste Wife Analogy & Unlettered Realization**

> **The Chaste Wife: Concealing Devotion vs. Showmanship:**  
> When a man entered his *bhajana-kuṭīr* making an ostentatious public display of crying and emotional ecstasy, Śrīla Gaura Kiśora Dāsa Bābājī immediately ordered him to leave:  
> *“One who has actually attained love of Godhead will never display the symptoms but will always keep them hidden from the general people, just as a chaste wife will become very embarrassed if she has to show any part of her body, and thus keeps her body very thoroughly covered.*  
> 
> *In the same way, when one has real devotion unto the Supreme Lord and becomes elevated to love of Godhead, he always feels embarrassed to exhibit the symptoms of ecstasy, and thus keeps such symptoms unmanifest. One cannot become a vaiṣṇava by becoming deceitful. One has to remain very simple in his or her behavior.”*  
>
> — From Amara Prabhura-kathā, a collection of articles written by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Prabhupada. Translated by Vyenkata Das Brahmachari.
>
> **Realization Beyond Mundane Literacy:**  
> Śrīla Prabhupāda recalled the profound spiritual realization of his grand-spiritual master:  
> *“You’ll be surprised to know that my spiritual master’s spiritual master was illiterate. And my spiritual master was the greatest learned scholar of his age. My grand-spiritual master’s name was Gour Kishore Das Babaji Maharaja. He was illiterate, but when you asked him some intricate question of spiritual significance, he would answer you immediately and very nicely. That is realization.”*
> HDG SP Lecture in Los Angeles. 12 January 1968

## Slide 8: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **The Mirror of Duplicity:** Principle 19 teaches that duplicity degrades human consciousness below animal life, and that modern conditioning trains us to be dishonest with ourselves. Where in our devotional life do we catch ourselves wearing a spiritual mask, managing our image, or rationalizing compromises?

2. **Spiritual Tourism vs. Internal Transformation:** Bhakti Tirtha Swami warns against the materialistic illusion that "the situation will always be better somewhere else." When service friction, boredom, or difficulties arise, do we seek external escapes, or do we have the simplicity to let Kṛṣṇa purify our heart right where we are?
