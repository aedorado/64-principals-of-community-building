## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem (Principle 27)
### **Wasting the Human Form in Complacency & Temporary Pursuits**

* **Longevity Without Devotion:**  
  "Although most people are concerned with longevity, simply trying to increase the duration of life is not most important. What is most important is the quality of one’s life. For instance, a tree can live for hundreds of years, but this is not a sign of an advanced life. If we live a life void of devotion, we will end up wasting our rare human form."

* **Complacency & Temporary Pursuits:**  
  "Since we will not stay in this world very long, we do not want to become complacent. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta did not say that success comes through money, fame, prestige, and so on; all such enjoyments are temporary."

## Slide 3: Principle 27 — Passing Through to a Glorious Departure
### **Using the Human Body to Surrender to God**

Principle 27: "We will not stay in this world for a long time. Only if this body falls singing the glory of Lord Sri Hari will our birth in this body be successful."

"As Srila Prabhupada writes in his introduction to the *Bhagavad-gita*, *'Every activity of the human being is to be considered a failure unless he inquires about the nature of the Absolute. Therefore those who begin to question why they are suffering or where they came from and where they shall go after death are proper students for understanding Bhagavad-gita.'* The real purpose of our human body is to use it for surrendering to God."

"Let nothing distract or disturb us because we are just passing through. We want to be fixed in chanting the glories of Lord Krishna at the time of our death. We will attain success if our bodies fall glorifying Lord Hari."

## Slide 4A: Living Example (Principle 27) — Jayānanda Prabhu’s Disappearance
### **Leaving the Body Hugging Śrīla Prabhupāda’s Chanting (*TKG Diary*)**

> **May 3, 1977 — Prabhupāda’s Tears:**  
> *“Today, we received word that Jayananda Prabhu had given up his body. When Prabhupada was informed he said, ‘Yes, sooner or later we must all go.’ Afterwards, tears came to His Divine Grace’s eyes. ‘He was one of my very best disciples... He used to drive me in the car, and while driving he would be chanting.’* When asked about his destination, Prabhupāda assured: *‘He is not lost. He will be promoted... But if he was thinking of Krishna, then he can have gone directly to Krishna-loka.’”*  
> 
> **May 11, 1977 — Hugging the Tape Recorder Until the Last Breath:**  
> *“Jayananda had been in great pain for months, but he never ceased serving Lord Jagannath and making arrangements for His Ratha-yātrā celebration. On his last day, the pain increased to the point that he could no longer even chant the holy name... At the very end, he hugged his tape recorder tightly, listening to his spiritual master’s chanting of Hare Krishna, and left his body.*  
> *Of his beloved disciple, Srila Prabhupada said, ‘I am very proud to have such a disciple. He has died gloriously. His death is wonderful and we should all follow his example.’ Prabhupada quoted Bhagavad-gītā 8:5 and read the purport in confirmation. Jayananda has gone to Vaikuntha to be with Krishna. His photo will be placed on the Ratha cart, and in all our temples we will hold a feast in his honor as we do on the other vaiṣṇava’s disappearance days. Jaya Jayananda!”*  
> 
> **June 13, 1977:** Looking at the *Back to Godhead* Ratha-yātrā cover photo of Lord Jagannātha with Jayānanda, *“Prabhupada took off his glasses and wiped tears from his eyes with his handkerchief.”*

*Source: TKG Diary / Sri Krishna Kathamrita Bindu, Issue No. 77*

## Slide 4B: Living Example (Principle 27) — Bhakta Kavi Gopāl Kṛṣṇa (Part I)
### **Remaining Calm Through Tragedy & Sending the Mind to Vṛndāvana**

> **Unruffled Amidst Bereavement (*Age 60*):**  
> Born in 1784 in Odisha in the line of Śrīla Śyāmānanda Prabhu, Bhakta Kavi Gopāl Kṛṣṇa lost his second son Haribandhu and then his son-in-law at age sixty. *“In spite of such misfortune, the poet remained calm and unruffled. Rather than lamenting, the poet increasingly engaged himself in the reading of Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta, in the worship of his iṣṭadeva [Sri Sri Radha Rasikaraja Krishna], and in remembering the divine pastimes of Sri Sri Radha Krishna.”*  
> 
> **Instructing His Own Mind:**  
> *brajabanaku māna jā chālire / jaya rādhe kṛṣṇa bolire*  
> *bhāḷanā ethire kichi nāhiṅ sukha / murukha sabuti dukhalare*  
> *nibhṛta kuñjare bije karicchanti / rahi dekha candra mukhare*  
> *“O mind! Go to the forest of Vrindavan, singing, ‘All glories to Sri Sri Radha and Krishna!’ You foolish mind, don’t worry, because there is no happiness here. Rather, this world is full of displeasure. Stay a while in Vrindavan and see the moonlike face of Radha Krishna, who are staying in a solitary grove... Stay with the sakhīs and mañjarīs, for they will help you by destroying all obstacles.”*

*Source: Bhakta Kavi Gopal Krishna by Fakir Mohan Das / Sri Krishna Kathamrita Bindu, Issue No. 367*

## Slide 4C: Living Example (Principle 27) — Bhakta Kavi Gopāl Kṛṣṇa (Part II)
### **His Final Meditation at Rādhā-Kuṇḍa & Last Words on the Wall**

> **Departing in *Padmāsana* (*Age 78, Phālguna 1862*):**  
> On the day he left this world, Gopāl Kṛṣṇa first completed his morning worship and began reciting the *mahā-mantra* on his *japa-mālā*, sitting in perfect *padmāsana* pose. He wrote a poem meditating upon his eternal spiritual form (*siddha-deha*) given by Śrī Gurudeva:  
> *“If I receive my spiritual body given by Sri Gurudeva, then going with that body I will stand near to Radha Kund... Seeing the trees and creepers in the forest on the bank of Radha Kund, I will call their names with affection, and paying obeisances to them I will embrace each and every one. Alas! Seeing the lotus footprints of Radha Krishna in the fine sand, I will immediately roll on the ground there. And looking upon the Lord’s wonderful form of eternal pleasure, I will feel unlimited joy and will stay there at his lotus feet.”*  
> 
> **Scripted on the Wall Before Leaving:**  
> After completing his daily quota of *japa*, he wrote his final couplet on the wall and departed:  
> *jīva jivābelaku hoilā saja rādhā-govinda bhaja*  
> **“O *jīva* soul! Now you are going to leave this world. Pray to Radha Govinda.”**

*Source: Bhakta Kavi Gopal Krishna by Fakir Mohan Das / Sri Krishna Kathamrita Bindu, Issue No. 367*

## Slide 5: Principle 28 — The Dust of Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī
### **Redirecting Our Desires & The Three Powerful Substances**

Principle 28: "The only desired object in our life is to collect the dust from the lotus feet of Sri Rupa Gosvami, who fulfilled the desire of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu."

"We are not desireless; we are full of desires. However, we want to desire the dust of the lotus feet of the great servants of the Lord who have surrendered everything to the Divine Couple. They carry a tremendous amount of bhakti... And we especially want to take shelter of the lotus feet of Rupa Gosvami. We are rupanugas or followers of Srila Rupa Gosvami. By his mercy, we are getting a wonderful opportunity to engage in devotional service and return back to the spiritual world."

"The dust of the feet of a devotee, the water that has washed the feet of a devotee, and the remnants of food left by a devotee are three very powerful substances. By rendering service to these three, one attains the supreme goal of ecstatic love for Krishna. In all the revealed scriptures this is loudly declared again and again." (*Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila*, 16.60–61)

## Slide 6: Living Example (Principle 28) 
### **Intoxicated by the Remnants & Foot-Water of Vaiṣṇavas (*CC Antya 16*)**

> **1. Simple Faith Above Caste (*Antya 16.6–9*):**  
> A relative of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī named Kālidāsa (*sarala viśvāsa*—very simple and pure in faith) took tremendous pleasure in honoring the remnants of Vaiṣṇavas, making no distinction between *brāhmaṇa*, *kṣatriya*, *vaiśya*, *śūdra*, or *yavana*—whenever he recognized someone as a Vaiṣṇava, he honored their remnants.  
> 
> **2. Mango Seeds from Jhaḍu Ṭhākura’s Refuse Ditch (*Antya 16.14–37*):**  
> Visiting Jhaḍu Ṭhākura—an exalted *mahā-bhāgavata* born in a lower *Śākadvīpī śūdra* (*bhuinmālī*) family—Kālidāsa gifted him a basket of sweet mangoes. After Jhaḍu Ṭhākura offered and honored them, the discarded skins and pits were thrown into a refuse ditch outside:  
> *“Thereafter, Kālidāsa went to the refuse hole outside Jhaḍu Ṭhākura’s house where the remnants had been thrown. He picked up the discarded skins and sucked mango seeds, and as he relished them again and again, he became intoxicated with ecstatic love of Kṛṣṇa.”*  
> 
> **3. Mahāprabhu Breaks His Rule (*Antya 16.44–58*):**  
> Later in Jagannātha Purī, although Lord Caitanya strictly forbade anyone from touching His feet or taking His foot-wash water (*pāda-udaka*), seeing Kālidāsa standing with thirsty eyes, He allowed him to drink three palmfuls—declaring:  
> *“O Kālidāsa, it is because of your genuine devotion to Kṛṣṇa that I have acted in this way toward you. Whoever possesses the great fortune of taking the remnants and foot dust of the Vaiṣṇavas is personally rewarded by Kṛṣṇa with the divine treasure of love of Godhead [prema-dhana].”*

*Source: Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Antya-līlā 16.6–58*

## Slide 7: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Passing Through vs. Getting Entangled:** Principle 27 urges: *"Let nothing distract or disturb us because we are just passing through."* What temporary anxieties, grudges, or material pursuits are currently absorbing your mental energy as if we were staying in this world forever?

2. **Awakening Greed for *Rūpānuga* Mercy:** *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* highlights three substances—the dust of a devotee’s feet, the water that washed their feet, and their food remnants—as direct gateways to ecstatic love. How can we replace superficial familiarity with genuine reverence and eagerness to serve the surrendered servants of Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī?
