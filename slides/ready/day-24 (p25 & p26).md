## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem (Principle 25)
### **The Disease of Accumulation & Distraction**

* **Creating Our Own Obstacles:**  
  "One of the principles that leads to falldown is excessive collecting. By investing our valuable time, energy, and intelligence in unnecessary and unwanted things, we create our own obstacles. We have this disease of accumulation."

* **Forgetting Our Primary Needs:**  
  "When we are too involved in collecting things that are not relevant to our genuine spiritual needs, it distracts us from focusing on our primary needs and spiritual growth. The more we pursue temporary concerns and commodities, the more we become forgetful and attached to the illusory energies. Maya tries very hard to keep the living entities distracted from pursuing sanatana-dharma."

---

## Slide 3: Principle 25 — Bhakti Is Elimination, Not Acquisition
### **Backing Away from the Superfluous Toward the Divine Couple**

Principle 25: "Our main disease is to collect objects that are not related to Krishna."

"Spiritual elevation is not so much about acquiring but about shedding. We want to shed anything that hinders our progress on the path of devotional service... the process of bhakti involves elimination. By backing away from superfluous things, we will be able to move towards the Divine Couple. As we keep our lives focused on Krishna, we will naturally accept things favorable for devotional service and reject unfavorable things."

## Slide 4A: Living Example (Principle 25) — The Cuttack Maṭha Incident (Part I)
### **False Pretexts for Collection & Shows of Devotion**

> **Exaggerated Claims & 300 Vests:**  
> *“At the Cuttack maṭha there was one sannyāsī whose methods of collecting were not very pleasing to Saraswati Thakur. Sometimes this sannyāsī would tell people that the maṭha had thousands and thousands of brahmacārīs, that they also ran a leper colony which had one thousand lepers, that they ran a students’ hostel, and that they fed the poor. All of this was, of course, untrue, and Saraswati Thakur did not approve.”*  
> 
> *“On one occasion, the same sannyāsī went to the market and collected many things from Marwaris... He told one such businessman, ‘We have three hundred brahmacārīs, so give three hundred vests,’ which he got. Being very elated... when he arrived at the gate of the maṭha, he started calling out in a loud voice, ‘Jaya! Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati Goswami Maharaja ki jaya!’ However, Srila Saraswati Thakur was so upset that he fasted that whole day.”*  
> 
> **Insincere Initiates for Show:**  
> *“On another occasion the same devotee arranged for many gṛhasthas from the Midnapur district to take hari-nāma initiation. However, Saraswati Thakur said they were disciples of that sannyāsī and not his. ‘I don’t need so many so-called disciples. He is bringing so many insincere people for show only,’ he said.”*

*Source: Sri Krishna Kathamrita Bindu (adapted from Śrī Śrīmad Bhakti Vikāsa Swami’s biography of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī)*

## Slide 4B: Living Example (Principle 25) — The Cuttack Maṭha Incident (Part II)
### **The Income-Tax Officer’s Car & Returning the Donations**

> **Intimidating Donors Under False Colors:**  
> *“This same sannyāsī once went to the income tax officer and said, ‘Please help us to collect from the Marwaris,’ knowing that the Marwaris were generally wealthy... However, the income tax officer refused, saying, ‘This is not proper.’ The sannyāsī then said, ‘Well at least let me take your car, now that I’ve come, so I can get back to the maṭha.’”*  
> 
> *“The income tax officer granted this, but the sannyāsī went back to the maṭha via the bazaar, and, without the income tax officer’s knowing, started collecting donations from the Marwaris. Seeing the income tax officer’s car, they felt there was some kind of threat, and so they gave profusely, including money, dhotis, and so many other things.”*  
> 
> **Angry Like Fire & Returning Everything:**  
> *“When Saraswati Thakur heard how he had made this collection he became angry like fire and arranged to have everything returned. He used to say, **‘karilām sannyāsī hoy, gelo vilāsī — I made them sannyāsīs but they’ve all become sense enjoyers.’**”*

*Source: Sri Krishna Kathamrita Bindu (adapted from Śrī Śrīmad Bhakti Vikāsa Swami’s biography of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī)*

## Slide 5: The Root of the Problem (Principle 26)
### **Our Cheating Propensity: Buying Off the Lord with Things**

* **Mistaking External Offerings for Surrender:**  
  "Krishna does not need anything; He is self-satisfied. Nevertheless, we cook for Him, bathe Him, dress Him, build beautiful buildings for Him, and distribute books and prasadam as a way to preach about Him."

* **Withholding Our Hearts:**  
  "Due to our cheating propensity, we are willing to give so many things to the Lord as long as we do not have to give our hearts. We try to ingratiate Krishna through our offerings, but it is not the same as giving our selves."

---

## Slide 6: Principle 26 — Peons of Mahāprabhu’s Mercy
### **Giving Our Hearts in Indebted Servitude**

Principle 26: "We have not come to this world to become an artisan of wood or stone; we are only peons of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s mercy."

"But what does He really want? He ultimately wants us to give our hearts and give up sense gratification. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that we are to be peons of the Lord’s mercy. A peon is one who is held in compulsory servitude to a master for the working out of a debt. He is trying to help us understand our position in relation to the Lord."

"Lord Caitanya has given us so much mercy by teaching us in the mood of a devotee how to serve Krishna (Himself). We are seriously indebted to Him and we are so blessed that He extends His mercy so profusely. We can never fully repay those special souls who connect us with Lord Caitanya’s mercy, but we can reflect constantly with gratitude, appreciation, and excitement."

## Slide 7: Living Example (Principle 26) — “I Will Not Leave One Penny for You”
### **Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī on Preserving Purity Over Property**

> *“Please preach the kīrtan of the Supreme Lord, even if in doing so you have to incur debt; then in order to pay off that debt, you will have to be even more engaged in sevā. When your creditors put pressure on you, you will be forced to beg more for alms. Again, as the pious householders will not give you alms unless your character and conduct are pure, you will be forced to preserve a pure lifestyle with great determination and care. **I will not leave one penny for you, and in that way in the future you won’t be able to indulge in laziness and you won’t be able to give up your devotional lives full of hari-kīrtan and hari-sevā.**”*  

*Source: Amṛta Vāṇī*

## Slide 8: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Acquiring vs. Shedding (*Atyāhāra*):** Principle 25 states that spiritual elevation is about *shedding* rather than acquiring, and that collecting things unrelated to Kṛṣṇa distracts us from our primary needs. Look honestly at your physical possessions, digital feeds, and social commitments—what unnecessary accumulations are slowing down your journey toward Kṛṣṇa?

2. **Giving Things vs. Giving the Heart:** Bhakti Tirtha Swami exposes our cheating propensity: *"We are willing to give so many things to the Lord as long as we do not have to give our hearts."* Where in our devotional life do we busy ourselves as "artisans" doing external service or donations to feel pious, while quietly keeping our pride and attachments untouched?
