## Slide 2: Q1A: Escaping Family Duties for *Sannyāsa* (Part I)
### **Exposing Escapism & Defining True Detachment**

**Question:** *"What should one do in a case where the husband has no desire to take care of his children or his wife, but feels he wants freedom to pursue sannyasa. He feels family life is attachment and is interfering with his advancement?"*

  *"Why don’t you get a broom and beat him over the head! He is not an example of detachment; rather, he is running away from responsibility. In some cases, a person is detached because they are bored with their old wife and with their tedious responsibilities. There were cases in which a grhastha would ask Srila Prabhupada for sannyasa, but he refused them because he understood the underlying reasons."*

  *"In some cases, a husband might act detached from his wife, but if we look closer, we will see that he has a strong attachment to other women and other forms of sense gratification... Detachment does not just mean detachment from the externals of sense gratification; it means attachment to the service and worship of Krishna. When that type of detachment occurs, then the person can offer more to any relationship."*

## Slide 3: Q1B: Escaping Family Duties for *Sannyāsa* (Part II)
### **Clear Arrangements & Honoring Commitments**

  *"If someone wants to take sannyasa but they have children, they have to make clear and healthy arrangements for the wife and children. In the past, some men would pursue the idea of sannyasa without giving any consideration to their wife and children. Then the husband would leave, take sannyasa, and travel all over the world while the family is left struggling. Then the wife gets angry at the husband and the movement, and in some cases, even leaves the movement. Renunciation should not be avoidance of responsibility."*

  *"However, they made a commitment and they should follow through. Some of the best householders in the movement are those who have learned how to deal with conflicts and who are completely aware of the illusory aspects of householder life. The husband and wife have grown to understand each other, and they understand the need to protect each other and work together in preaching and service. People who have a romantic conception of marriage will want a divorce when it does not unfold according to their own ideas."*

## Slide 4A: Q1 Living Example (Part I)
### **Śrīla Prabhupāda on Duty Before *Sannyāsa***

* **Not Creating Havoc by "Writing for *Sannyāsa*" (Letter to Danavir, Dec 17, 1972):**  
  *“That decision you have made. That is great responsibility, and that should not become so light matter that anyone may think, Oh, let me get married and if I don't like my wife, or there is anything difficulty, I will write Prabhupada for taking sannyasa, finished. Never mind wife, let her go to hell. That is not very nice proposal. Married life is serious business. If you have taken wife, you must be completely responsible for her throughout your life... That is not our business, to create havoc, no. If wife is very strong, she will appreciate if you take sannyasa, but if there is question at all, that should be avoided. Just like I never liked my wife, but I knew it was my duty to stick until my sons were grown-up, then I left.”*

* **Chastising a Husband Leaving Wife and Infant (Letter to Mahesvari, May 26, 1974):**  
  *“I am very sorry that your husband Mahatma das has taken my letter to him in the wrong way and has told you to get married to another man although you are only 20 years old and have a 7 month old son. This is a most irresponsible and nonsensical proposal on his part. I never wrote or intended that he should leave you. He can stay out and distribute books on sankirtana, but for that he can remain grhastha, nor does it means he perpetually remains on sankirtana party forgetting his responsibilities. You write that you badly need your husbands instruction and security, therefore, this is natural, so he may go on sankirtana but he must also spend time with you as you require, not in the mood of sense gratification but for cooperation in Krsna Consciousness.”*

## Slide 4B: Q1 Living Example (Part II)
### **Marriage Is Not an Impediment — *Sannyāsa* by Action, Not by Dress**

* **Calling Family Life an "Impediment to Advancement" (Letter to Madhukara, Jan 4, 1973):**  
  *“You say that your 'association together was hindering your advancement.' But Krsna consciousness marriage system should not be taken in that way, that if there is any botheration that means something is hindering my spiritual progress, no. Once it is adopted, the grhastha life, even it may be troublesome at times, it must be fulfilled as my occupational duty... But if it becomes so easy for me to get married and then leave my wife, under excuse of married life being an impediment to my own spiritual progress, that will not be very good at all. That is misunderstanding of what is advancement in spiritual life... Because the real fact is that if there is any difficulty with others, that is my lack of Krsna consciousness, not theirs. Is this clear?”*

* **Reassuring a Wife Anxious Over Her Husband's *Sannyāsa* (Letter to Indira, Aug 15, 1971):**  
  *“So if you are afraid of your husband's taking sannyasa, I shall not give him sannyasa order at any time. Be rest assured. Your husband is already a householder sannyasi because he has no other business than to serve Krishna. You can be living peacefully with husband and children and always engaged in Krishna Consciousness. One should become sannyasa by action, not by dress.”*

## Slide 5: Q2A: Our Position in the *Paramparā* (Part I)
### **Beads on the String & a Tremendous Spiritual Family**

**Question:** *"As disciples and grand-disciples of Srila Prabhupada, can you elaborate on our position in the parampara?"*

* **Linked to the Entire Chain:**  
  *"We can see the parampara as a chain. Reflect on the position of a single bead on your string of japa beads. That one bead is connected to the whole chain by the string that runs through each bead... When we accept a bona fide guru, we are actually making the connection that will give us the blessings of the whole parampara if we follow properly."*

* **A Spiritual Heritage & Entire Family:**  
  *"It is not that we just get the shelter of guru and the relationship stops there. We get the shelter of the spiritual master who then arranges to connect us with a tremendous spiritual family. It is wonderful to think about what Srila Prabhupada has given us. He gave us an amazing heritage. We are receiving blessings from both our immediate family as well as from the previous acaryas. When a culture is proper, it always involves more than just the child and the parent; it involves the entire family."*

---

## Slide 6: Q2B: Our Position in the *Paramparā* (Part II)
### ***Dīkṣā*, *Śikṣā*, and Readiness to Receive Orders**

* **Community Support Through *Dīkṣā* and *Śikṣā* Gurus:**  
  *"In communities where many gurus and leaders regularly visit, those devotees will often have strong relationships not only with their diksa-guru but also with various siksa-gurus. They feel that many of the leaders are there to assist them, which helps them feel a stronger connection to the whole movement. In terms of our diksa-guru, sometimes there is just an automatic connection because it is a connection from a previous lifetime."*

* **The Essential Test Before Accepting a Guru:**  
  *"Most importantly, an aspiring disciple should ask, ‘Is this the person from whom I am ready to receive orders? Is this the person who I feel can connect me with Krishna?’ This is one of the major considerations when an individual chooses a guru. The person should feel that Krishna is coming through in a way that inspires him or her to receive an order. A disciple should not just accept a guru due to sentimental considerations."*

## Slide 7: Q2 Living Example
### **Śrīla Prabhupāda on the *Paramparā* Current & Spiritual Family**

* **A Spiritual Family of Thousands (Letter to Mukunda, Nov 11, 1973):**  
  *“And, Narottama Das Thakura says: tandera carana sevi bhakta-sane vasa / janame janame haya, ei abhilasa: 'To serve the feet of the acaryas in association of the devotees is my desire birth after birth.' Why I have opened this Society. I was living with four children, and now I have 4,000. There is no good in living alone.”*

* **The Law of Disciplic Succession (Letter to Haṁsadūta, Dec 10, 1975):**  
  *“Keep trained up very rigidly and then you are bona fide Guru, and you can accept disciples on the same principle. But as a matter of etiquette it is the custom that during the lifetime of your Spiritual master you bring the prospective disciples to him, and in his absence or disappearance you can accept disciples without any limitation. This is the law of disciplic succession. I want to see my disciples become bona fide Spiritual Master and spread Krishna consciousness very widely, that will make me and Krishna very happy.”*

## Slide 8: Q3A: *Sādhu, Śāstra, and Guru* in Surrender (Part I)
### **Balanced Surrender vs. Fanatical Hero Worship**

**Question:** *"How essential are sadhu, sastra, and guru in the process of surrender? Can a person who only takes shelter of guru make proper progress?"*

* **Surrendering to All Three Pillars:**  
  *"Most people do not surrender—not fully. They read some scripture at their convenience, associate with sadhus who support their ideas, and go to a guru when he gives a pleasing instruction, but at other times, they avoid all three... Surrender means that we surrender to all the necessary factors—sadhu, sastra, and guru. Someone who serves the spiritual master without including sadhu and sastra is just involved in fanaticism. That same person will even viciously reject his or her own guru at a later time due to such myopic conceptions."*

* **When Hero Worship Turns Into Offense:**  
  *"Sometimes the disciples who become the greatest offenders are the ones who were the greatest fanatics in their service to the spiritual master... However, since they were into hero worship, they became serious offenders when something shattered their vision of their hero. Perhaps the guru suddenly gets another secretary and puts the disciple in another position, which causes the disciple to completely lose faith and become blasphemous... We can see how the false ego can trick devotees when they are not properly grounded."*

## Slide 9: Q3B: *Sādhu, Śāstra, and Guru* in Surrender (Part II)
### **The Substance of Deep Surrender**

* **Not Sentimentalism, But Meticulous Dedication:**  
  *"We have to have meticulous dedication, but not as sentimentalists. Sometimes people such as the yogis or jnanis think of bhakti-yoga as the easy path for sentimental people. They think it is all about emotion and surrender, but surrender is a very deep thing."*

* **The Essential Qualities for Full Surrender:**  
  *"You cannot surrender sufficiently without knowledge, submissiveness, humility, or deep faith. These are essential qualities which allow a person to reach the point of full surrender."*

## Slide 10: Q3 Living Example
### **Śrīla Prabhupāda on *Sādhu, Śāstra, Guru* & Association Through Service**

* **The Threefold Shelter to Conquer Doubts (Letter to Madhusūdana, Jan 16, 1970):**  
  *“You say that you will do as I say and you also prayed to Madhusudana to slay the demon of your doubt but unless you act according to the direction of Madhusudana how can Madhusudana or Madhusudana's father help you. You are with us practically for the last four years and still if you think you cannot surrender to Krishna without any doubt, then what kind of progress you have made by this time? So this Krishna science can be understood through the process of sincere service, and so far any doubt which may sometimes arise in our mind there are the devotees, the Scriptures and the Spiritual Master. Did you discuss all your doubts with your contemporary Godbrothers and devotees? ... You have accepted this chain of authorities, now you are feeling doubts means certainly you are being attacked by Maya. So please take care and chant Hare Krishna regularly without any offense.”*

* **The Bug on the King's Lap vs. Association Through Service (Letter to Satādhanya, Feb 20, 1972):**  
  *“So far personal association with the Guru is concerned, I was only with my Guru Maharaja four or five times, but I have never left his association, not even for a moment. Because I am following his instructions, I have never felt any separation. There are some of my Godbrothers here in India who had constant personal association with Guru Maharaja, but who are neglecting his orders. This is just like the bug who is sitting on the lap of the king. He may be very puffed-up by his position, but all he can succeed in doing is biting the king. Personal association is not so important as association through service.”*

## Slide 11: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Detachment or Avoidance?** Bhakti Tirtha Swami reminds us that true detachment is *"attachment to the service and worship of Krishna"*—which empowers us to offer *more* to every relationship rather than running away. Where in our family or community duties are we tempted to call boredom or avoidance "renunciation"?

2. **Grounded Surrender vs. Hero Worship:** Serving *guru* without *sādhu* and *śāstra* breeds fanaticism that can turn into offense the moment our personal position changes. How do we actively keep our devotion anchored in all three—*sādhu, śāstra, and guru*—so our service is never tied to ego, prestige, or convenience?
