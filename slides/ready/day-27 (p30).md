## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem (Principle 30)
### **Temporary Alignments & Unpaid Cosmic Debts**

* **Over-Attachment to Temporary Connections:**  
  "Some of us are strongly attached to our relatives. It is natural; they brought us into this world. In each birth, we have a completely different alignment. We have been mothers many times and fathers many times. We have had so many other connections to different living entities."

* **Obligations We Cannot Repay on Our Own:**  
  "Religionists do not realize how many debts they owe just by receiving a body. They owe the demigods, the ancestors, the sun, the moon, etc. We depend on so many agents for all of our daily activities, including eating and breathing. If we are not devoted, we then have many obligations to many other living beings."

---

## Slide 3: Principle 30 — Instructions for Ultimate Benefit
### **Covering Every Debt Through Genuine Devotion**

Principle 30: "In every birth one gets a father and a mother. But one may not get instructions for his ultimate benefit."

"We have some karmic responsibility because they have somehow helped us obtain a body in which we can take to devotional service. We do have a debt, but by engaging in our devotional service, we cover all our debts. Normally a person has a karmic responsibility, but devotional service covers our debt—if we are actually devoted."

"Therefore, we do not have to get overly excited or attached to this temporary connection that only lasts for a few short years. We do not want to deny the relationship, but we also do not want to see it as the all-in-all."

---

## Slide 4: Living Example (Principle 30 — Beyond Blind Ancestral Loyalty)
### **The Ancestral Well (*Upākhyāne Upadeśa*, Ch. 24)**

* **Fatal Attachment to Biological Lineage:**  
  A village pandit worshiped his departed father as God incarnate (*pitā svargaḥ pitā dharmaḥ...*) and forbade his family from drinking any water except from his great-grandfather's well. Even when the well became contaminated and killed his sons, grandsons, and wife—with clean tube wells and the Gaṅgā nearby—he sat on the well to block authorities from closing it.

* **Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's Purport (*Kula-guru* vs. *Sad-guru*):**  
  *“When one’s forefathers took initiation into a particular goswami line, at that time the water in that well was clean and good for drinking... However, in time some deviation entered. Despite this, the members of the family, in the name of adherence to their forefathers, continued to drink what had by then become poisonous water from the same well.”* Treating ancestral tradition as a substitute for a bona fide *sad-guru* never brings eternal auspiciousness.

## Slide 5: The Root of the Problem (Principle 31)
### **External Workaholism & Judging by Appearances**

* **Hard Work Without Devotional Consciousness:**  
  "Sometimes we see devotees who work very hard, and sometimes we see materialists who work even harder. There are some materialists who work six or seven days a week... As devotees, we do not just want to become workaholics because many people work. There are criminals who work harder than anyone else. They can be extremely clever and regulated in putting many hours into their deviations."

* **Superficial Judgments & Holding Back:**  
  "We have to be careful about judging each other. The larger sum of money might seem to be the better offering, but not really... One person might have so much facility but never use it resourcefully while another person has very little but uses it fully."

---

## Slide 6: Principle 31 (Part I) — Milk vs. Lime Water
### **Consciousness Over Work & Internal Alignment**

Principle 31: "Although the activities of a devotee and the misdemeanor of a pseudo-devotee appear to be alike, there is a heaven and hell of difference—like milk and lime water."

"What is the difference between the work of a devotee and the work of a materialist? The distinction relates to consciousness and to the purpose of the work... Although surrender never depends on work, we want our internal life to correspond with our external life. A person might try to solely think of Krishna without doing any service, but if he or she has the ability, the person should act. Krishna, who resides in the heart, knows the degree that we are holding back. He is watching to see what we are going to do with what He has given."

---

## Slide 7: Principle 31 (Part II) — Measuring by What We Hold Back
### **Kṛṣṇa Wants Our Hearts, Not Material Calculations**

"One person, for instance, might have millions of dollars and give tens of thousands of dollars as a donation. Another person might give one hundred dollars, but only had two hundred dollars. In one sense, the second person made a greater sacrifice..."

"The process of devotional service does not follow the rules of the material world. Krishna wants our hearts. As we can see, surrender is not really based on what we are giving or how we are serving; it is based on what we are holding back."

---

## Slide 8: Principle 31 (Part III) — Heart Over External Volume
### **Five Books vs. One Hundred Books & Sudāmā’s Chipped Rice**

"We have to be careful about judging each other. One book distributor might sell fifty to one hundred books a day, but focuses primarily on the money and his or her own prestige. Another person, who is trying very hard, simply distributes five books a day. However, this devotee consistently meditates on his service to guru and Krishna, and is really trying to help every single person he meets. The second individual is actually making more advancement because he is more surrendered to the actual service."

"Of course, it is good if a person can surrender and also have good results, but it is not always the same because we have different capacities. One person might have so much facility but never use it resourcefully while another person has very little but uses it fully."

"We see this happen in the life of Sudama Vipra. He did not have very much, but he offered Krishna a little chipped rice, which pleased the Lord immensely. Although he was poor, he was rich with devotion; therefore, Krishna fully reciprocated."

## Slide 9A: Living Example (Principle 31 — Part I)
### **Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār - When Irreligion Becomes Religion**

* **The Lord Neglected While Materialists Enjoyed:**  
  Arriving at Śrī Raṅganātha's temple with four disciples possessing extraordinary powers—**Tarka Cūḍāmaṇi** (unbeatable debater), **Dvāra-unmocaka** (opened any locked door), **Chāyāgraha** (immobilized anyone touched by his shadow), and **Jaloparicara** (walked on water)—Tirumaṅgai saw the temple crumbling, surrounded by bushes, its courtyard filled with leather shoes, and servants visiting only once a day out of fear of tigers and jackals.

* **Rejected by Rich Thieves, Resolving to Recover the Lord's Wealth:**  
  When the penniless saint begged alms from wealthy materialists living in gorgeous palaces, they called him a thief and taunted, *“Why does a sādhu need money?”* Resolving to reclaim the Lord's wealth from those enjoying it in illusion, Tirumaṅgai and his four disciples acted like a gang of robbers: Tarka Cūḍāmaṇi entangled them in debate, Dvāra-unmocaka unlocked their treasuries, Chāyāgraha froze everyone, and Jaloparicara crossed deep moats to bring back the wealth.

  — Weekly Gaudiya, Vol. 2. Adapted from the English translation
of Bhumipati Das, from Brahmana and Vaishnava. Vrajaraj Press.
Vrindavan. 1999

## Slide 9B: Living Example (Principle 31 — Part II)
### **Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār - When Irreligion Becomes Religion**

* **No Personal Enjoyment — Building for Śrī Raṅganātha Alone:**  
  With the recovered wealth, Tirumaṅgai brought expert artists from many provinces and built a magnificent seven-story temple. Yet he himself *“only ate what he cooked once a day, after offering it to the Lord”*—remaining a self-controlled *gosvāmī* with *“no desire at all for personal enjoyment or fame.”*

* **The *Smārta* vs. *Hari-jana* Conclusion:**  
  *“In the vision of a smārta, a moralist, the above-mentioned activities of Tirumangai Alwar are acts of robbery... According to the hari-janas, devotees, this plundering act of Tirumangai is devotional service performed in the mood of being humbler than a straw in the street, just as the burning of Lanka by Hanuman is an example of devotional service to the spiritual master... The reason is that in these activities there is no tinge of material enjoyment or liberation.”*

  — Weekly Gaudiya, Vol. 2. Adapted from the English translation

## Slide 10: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Debts Covered Without Denying Relationships:** Principle 30 teaches us neither to deny our temporary family ties nor to make them the "all-in-all," reminding us that genuine devotion repays every cosmic debt. Where do we still allow biological guilt or temporary family expectations to eclipse instructions for our ultimate spiritual benefit?

2. **What Are We Holding Back?** Bhakti Tirtha Swami cuts deep: *"Surrender is not really based on what we are giving or how we are serving; it is based on what we are holding back."* Where do we hide behind impressive outward numbers or "workaholism" while holding back our vulnerability, trust, or full heart from Kṛṣṇa—and how can we stop judging devotees by external quantity?
