## Slide 2: Q1 (Part A): Minimizing Truth vs. Planting Seeds

“Question: Aren’t we guilty of minimizing the truth if we don’t give the full message of Krishna consciousness in every possible situation?”

“There is a difference between minimizing the truth and planting seeds that set a foundation for people to later receive more. We minimize the truth when we have an opportunity to speak, but refrain due to intimidation. There were times when Srila Prabhupada spoke directly, and times when he did not preach philosophy at all, but simply treated a person kindly—such as George Harrison’s gardener, who remembered him fondly. Sometimes our examples speak louder than our words.”

“Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura knew the Bible, the Koran, the Torah, Shakespeare, Yeats, and Eliot, and even used a pen name for his books to reach the sahajiyas in their own language. Srila Prabhupada told us to solve material problems with spiritual solutions. We want the public to recognize we are not a weird sectarian group, but people with a higher understanding about life. Preaching is powerful, and our message is strong, but it has to connect—if we have good medicine, but no one wants to take it, what good will it do?”

---

## Slide 3: Q1 (Part B): Leadership — Vision With Love

“When people have vision without love, they will be motivated for the wrong reasons. Conversely, when people have love without vision, nothing gets accomplished. There has to be vision with love so devotees become motivated and enthused; otherwise, they lose enthusiasm and want to leave due to boredom and dissatisfaction. A leader should know his constituents as a mother knows her children.”

“We cannot always give everyone what they want, but the closer we come to engaging people according to their propensities, the better. If a devotee with brahminical qualities is doing mainly vaisya work collecting money day after day, or a ksatriya lacks a project to manage, they lose enthusiasm. Who is so surrendered that they can continue indefinitely doing something that does not stimulate them?”

“Unlike paid workers who tolerate a boss for a paycheck, our devotees are volunteering their services. Leaders must enthuse them and show genuine care. Then, even when devotees feel distraught, they think: *‘My leader cares for me so much that I don’t want to disappoint him.’*”

## Slide 4: Q1 Living Examples
### **Śrīla Bhaktivinoda, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta & Śrīla Prabhupāda**

“Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta were the foremost experts. Before Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s time, a renunciate would not cross the ocean or even ride in a car; they would have been considered fallen. But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had his disciples use cars and sent them to England to preach. Similarly, Srila Prabhupada used technology to establish Krishna consciousness worldwide.”

“Srila Prabhupada took the best from the West and the best from the East—comparing India to a lame man and the West to a blind man; combining the two creates something very powerful. Our temples in Bombay and Vrndavana are gorgeous because of their spirituality, diversity, and opulence, featuring first-class theaters, restaurants, and guest facilities. Even those not initially interested in spirituality come, and due to association, begin discovering our lifestyle.”

“This is weaponry. It is not minimizing our principles; it is maximizing our ability to reach and involve people. Srila Prabhupada explained that we should be ready to live in a small hut, but then who will come? Many would be timid. Therefore, we want to represent Krishna consciousness in a way that will attract people rather than repel them.”

## Slide 5: Q2: When Service Projects Fail — Intellect vs. Surrender
### **Over-Endeavoring vs. The Prayer of the Servant**

“Question: What if we have a strong desire to serve Krishna by working on a specific project, but no matter how hard we try, it just does not work out?”

“It could indicate that we are over-endeavoring for a material result rather than a spiritual goal. If a project does not work after repeated endeavors, it could be a sign we are not taking the healthiest approach. We should be in a prayerful state: *‘Krishna, if my plans do not follow Your desire, then don’t let them manifest. Please put me in a situation that helps me to do the right thing in the right way.’* If we act solely on our own intelligence, we will only meet with frustration.”

“Sometimes we see that the most intelligent devotees fall into maya because they embraced Krishna consciousness on an intellectual platform (*jñāna*) and not from the heart. When something does not rationally fit into their lives, everything gets thrown out because they cannot weather tests of faith. Krishna says in *Bhagavad-gītā* (18.55):  
*bhaktyā mām abhijānāti / yāvān yaś cāsmi tattvataḥ*  
*tato māṁ tattvato jñātvā / viśate tad-anantaram*  
*(One can understand Me as I am only by devotional service, and thus enter into the kingdom of God.)*”

## Slide 6: Q2 Living Example — Kṛṣṇa’s Plan vs. Our Schemes
### **Sincere Endeavor, Apparent Failure & Accepting Control**

> **1. Kṛṣṇa’s Plan Above All:**  
> *“So far your thinking about things and making so many plans, that is all right, but our first business is Krishna's plan. That is the only plan, and no matter how many schemes we employ to adjust things in this material world, if they have no solid basis in Krishna's plan, they remain merely dreams of phantasmagoria... So the point is to make use of what we already have. That is most important, and if Krishna sees that we are using it properly for pleasing Him in loving attitude of devotional service, then Krishna gives every facility in reciprocation of love.”* — **Letter to Mahātmā (April 10, 1972)**  
> 
> **2. Kṛṣṇa Approves When He Wills:**  
> *“You have tried so hard to find a new temple there but Krishna did not approve so there was no temple. But now that Krishna wills it with simply a flick of the eye a temple has come to us, better even than we were hoping for. So this is what is meant that Krishna is Controller and we are controlled. When we simply remember to accept the control of Krishna and not to act independently then all fortune is automatically present.”* — **Letter to Brahmānanda (December 11, 1968)**  
> 
> **3. Effort Without Fault (*Yatne Kṛte Yadi Na Sidhyati*):**  
> *“Go on doing like that, and our business is to try our best. Result we leave it for consideration of Krishna, and we shall not be disappointed whether the result is favorable or unfavorable. Actually there cannot be any unfavorable result, because we are serving Krishna... In Sanskrit it is said Yatne Krite Yadina Sidehati Kotra Dosah. That means, in spite of our best efforts, if things do not come successful, then there is no fault on our side. That should be the principle of our preaching, we shall try our best to convince others in Krishna Consciousness, and Krishna will be pleased, that is our gain.”* — **Letter to Kṛṣṇa Devī & Dinesh Chandra (September 26, 1968)**

## Slide 7: Q2 Living Example — Beyond Intellect & Personal Ambition
### **The Prayerful Mood & Spiritual Platform**

> **1. Praying to Be Forced into Surrender:**  
> *“Yes, you can pray to Krishna—Krishna may be prayed that I am unwilling to become Krishna Conscious, so you can force me to become so. You put me under certain circumstances so that I may be forced to accept Krishna Consciousness. You can pray like that. It is Krishna's special favor and mercy that He sometimes forces a devotee to surrender fully to Him.”* — **Letter to Harer Nāma (November 13, 1968)**  
> 
> **2. Depending on Grace Above Intellect:**  
> *“We are working neither on the sensual platform, nor on the mental platform, nor on the intellectual platform. We are working completely on the spiritual platform... Anyone trying to serve Krishna from the material platform of body, mind and intelligence cannot appreciate this fact. One has to become free from the contamination of matter, represented in gross and subtle forms as body, mind, intelligence and ego... Therefore, we should more depend to receive His Grace than to depend on mental or intellectual activities.”* — **Letter to Mohinī Mohana (May 22, 1969)**  
> 
> **3. Guarding Against the Poison of Personal Ambition:**  
> *“So these two things are always side by side—Maya and Krsna—Krsna is service and Maya is sense gratification, so every moment we are prone to be subjugated by either of them. Our duty is therefore to be very, very careful. The poison is personal ambition. So everyone has the chance, therefore one should not be complacent.”* — **Letter to a Disciple (October 16, 1970)**

## Slide 8: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Vision With Love & Motherly Care:** Bhakti Tirtha Swami warns that *"vision without love"* leads to wrong motives, while *"love without vision"* accomplishes nothing—and reminds leaders that devotees are volunteers, not paid employees. Where in our community do we overlook people's natural propensities (*brāhmaṇa*, *kṣatriya*, *vaiśya*) and let burnout replace inspiration?

2. **When Plans Collapse — Ego vs. Surrender:** Have you ever poured months of effort into a personal or devotional plan that Kṛṣṇa repeatedly blocked? Looking back, can you recognize Kṛṣṇa’s hand protecting you from *jñāna*-driven over-endeavor, and how easy is it for you today to sincerely pray: *“If my plans do not follow Your desire, don’t let them manifest”*?
