**Day 07: Offering All Glory to the Original Source**
Systematic Study of Principle 5: Uprooting pride, the danger of leadership proprietorship, and the safety of the transparent medium.

**Speaker:** HH Bhakti Dhira Damodara Swami Maharaja
**Course Text:** *Reflections on Sacred Teachings V* (Principle 5)

## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem
### **Why Do We Get Disturbed When We Don't Get Enough Praise?**

"It is very materialistic when we have people in the devotee community who so eagerly want to get credit for their activities, and who so eagerly want to claim responsibility."

"Some people are so much into glorification that they get disturbed when they do not get enough praise. Therefore, when a little praise does come, they wonder why it took so long... The negative influences of false ego and pride can create havoc in a devotee’s life."

---

## Slide 3: The Solution

Principle 5: "Rupanuga devotees offer all glory to the original source instead of depending on their own strength."

"Whenever Srila Prabhupada received glorification, he would react in the opposite way by giving all the credit to his spiritual master. He would explain how any successes he had achieved simply resulted from his loyal adherence to the instructions of his spiritual master."

---

## Slide 4: From Distinction to Gross Agitation

"Materialistic people and neophytes are so quick to let everyone know that they are the ones. When people get their Nobel prizes, their Oscars, their degrees, they say, 'It’s about time. They should have given it to me last year.'"

"Identifying with the results is very dangerous. To the degree that people claim the credits and accept proprietorship, they will develop more false ego. And the expression of the ego is just subtle sex, which means that too much desire for distinction, adoration, and self-glorification then leads to gross agitation and often falldown."

"People who want to claim the achievements will later want to claim the enjoyment."

---

## Slide 5: The Trap of Leadership Pride

"Embezzlement happens when leaders begin to think that the assets they are handling actually belong to them, keeping their own gratification in mind."

"As they deal with people, they begin to think that they are God’s gift to the world, or they begin to feel that the many accomplishments have happened due to their own strength, insight, and intelligence: 'Yes, I’ve helped you; therefore, you owe me.'"

"Most people in leadership are generally cheaters because they get caught up in these gross or subtle traps. It slows down any type of progress because it involves manipulation and exploitation."

---

## Slide 6: Upholding the Sacred Trust of Leadership
### **Protecting the Vulnerable**

"If the person who offered the help tries to own the gratitude of their dependents, he or she will end up exploiting those very people who came for protection and guidance."

"It is natural for a person in a superior position to have leverage, dominance, and control over others, but such a person must be able to check the exploitive tendency as others begin to feel that kind of connection."

"When people come for help, they leave themselves open. They view the authority as a big brother or parent. If the caretaker then exploits those people who have become vulnerable, heavy karmic consequences will follow."

---

## Slide 7A: The Living Example — Śrī Jayānanda Thākur (Part 1)
### **Avoiding Praise Like the Plague**

> **Running from Fame & The "Most Fallen" Game:**  
> "Humility was certainly Jayānanda's most prominent quality. He treated everyone as his superior, even new devotees. Although his service was glorious, he never wanted any glory. **He avoided praise like the plague.**
> 
> Devotees were always playing this funny game: we would praise him, and he would tell us how fallen he was. Praise naturally would spring to our lips, but we could not praise him, or he would just walk away."

> **Renunciation Without the Trappings:**  
> "He was truly in the renounced order, without all the trappings. He didn't want the attention. He didn't want people to bow down when he entered a room, nor the praise of a sannyāsī. Jayānanda was too busy being our father and mother and doing all the needful things."

*Sources: Remembering Jayananda by Kalakantha Dasa & Jayananda Thakur ACBSP by Vishoka Dasa*

---

## Slide 7B: The Living Example — Śrī Jayānanda Thākur (Part 2)
### **The Pūjārī of Guru's Grace (100% Credit to Prabhupāda)**

> **The Analogy of the Pūjārī (Servant of Servants):**  
> "Just as a good pūjārī performs his service on the altar in such a way that he doesn’t draw any attention to himself but allows the full focus to remain on the Deities, in the same way Jayānanda does the needful without any flair and attention to his own personal needs. He preferred to work under other authorities, so that he could truly be the servant of the servants."

> **Endowed Only by Guru's Grace:**  
> "An advanced devotee never claims to have his own strength, but always knows that it is only by the guru's grace that he has any shakti at all. This was totally Jayānanda, through and through. **He never took credit for anything; he always gave all the credit to Śrīla Prabhupāda.**
> 
> He spoke more about Prabhupāda than Kṛṣṇa. Never for a moment did he think that his good fortune was due to any effort of his own."

*Sources: Radha-Damodara Vilasa by Vaiyasaki Dasa & Jayananda Thakur ACBSP by Vishoka Dasa*

## Slide 8: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. Exposing the Desire for Credit: Why is it so attractive for our false ego to claim ownership over successful projects or beautiful service? How can we catch this tendency before we start demanding praise or feeling neglected?

2. The Mindset of the Caretaker: Bhakti Tirtha Swami warns that leaders who exploit those under their care face heavy consequences. How can senior devotees and managers maintain a healthy boundary to protect those who are vulnerable and open to them?

---

## Slide 9: From Hearing to Living
### **An Immediate Heart Offering**

Don’t just hear it — practice it.

**The 60-Second Credit Audit:**
1. **The Honest Look:** Silently bring to mind one service or skill where your false ego naturally loves getting noticed, appreciated, or praised.
2. **The Reality Check:** Ask yourself honestly: *“Without whose unseen sacrifice, guidance, or background support would my service be completely impossible?”*

**Live Zoom Chat: Pass the Credit to the Source**  
In the chat, name that service and publicly honor the devotee whose unseen contribution made your service possible:

> *“I often get appreciation for [Service/Project], but the truth is: without [Name]’s [unseen help / sacrifice / training], it would have never been possible!”*

***Kartik Sadhana: The Transparent Medium Rule — For the rest of Kartik, whenever you receive praise, appreciation, or credit for any service, do not absorb it. Instantly deflect it upward to your spiritual master and Śrīla Prabhupāda, and outwardly glorify one other devotee who assisted or inspired you.***
