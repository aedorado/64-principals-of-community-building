### **Sacred Community Course**

**Day 07: Offering All Glory to the Original Source**
Systematic Study of Principle 5: Uprooting pride, the danger of leadership proprietorship, and the safety of the transparent medium.

**Speaker:** HH Bhakti Dhira Damodara Swami Maharaja
**Course Text:** *Reflections on Sacred Teachings V* (Principle 5)

---

## Slide 2: Principle 5 — Offering All Glory to the Original Source

Principle 5: "Rupanuga devotees offer all glory to the original source instead of depending on their own strength."

"It is very materialistic when we have people in the devotee community who so eagerly want to get credit for their activities. Whenever Srila Prabhupada received glorification, he would react in the opposite way by giving all the credit to his spiritual master. He would explain how any successes he had achieved simply resulted from his loyal adherence to the instructions of his spiritual master."

---

## Slide 3: The Neophyte Desire for Adoration

"Materialistic people and neophytes are so quick to let everyone know that they are the ones. Some people are so much into glorification that they get disturbed when they do not get enough praise. Therefore, when a little praise does come, they wonder why it took so long."

"When people get their Nobel prizes, their Oscars, their degrees, they say, 'It’s about time. They should have given it to me last year.'"

"Identifying with the results is very dangerous. If one claims the results of activities, one will also have to claim the karma that comes along with that. The negative influences of false ego and pride can create havoc in a devotee’s life."

---

## Slide 4: The Desire for Distinction

"To the degree that people, especially in positions of authority, claim the credits and accept proprietorship, they will develop more false ego. And the expression of the ego is just subtle sex, which means that too much desire for distinction, adoration, and self-glorification then leads to gross agitation and often falldown."

"People who want to claim the benefits or the achievements will later want to claim the enjoyment. They will feel that they are entitled to stimulation and additional credits because they have accepted responsibility."

---

## Slide 5: The Trap of Embezzlement and Pride

"For these reasons, some religious or secular leaders are more prone to embezzlement and illicit affairs. These leaders begin to think that the assets they are handling actually belong to them, keeping their own gratification in mind."

"As they deal with people, they may begin to feel that the many accomplishments have happened due to their own strength, insight, and intelligence. The accomplishments may in fact be good, and people may tell the leader, 'You’ve helped me so much. You are so wonderful.'"

"If the person starts to think, 'Yes, I’ve helped you; therefore, you owe me,' after a while, they will start trying to enjoy or experience that which they oversee. Most people in leadership are generally cheaters because they get caught up in these sorts of gross or subtle traps. It slows down any type of progress because it involves manipulation and exploitation."

---

## Slide 6: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. Exposing the Desire for Credit: Why is it so attractive for our false ego to claim ownership over successful projects or beautiful service? How can we catch this tendency before we start demanding praise or feeling neglected?

2. The Mindset of the Caretaker: Bhakti Tirtha Swami warns that leaders who exploit those under their care face heavy consequences. How can senior devotees and managers maintain a healthy boundary to protect those who are vulnerable and open to them?

---

## Slide 7: Upholding the Sacred Trust of Leadership

"It is natural that people who receive help from a leader will feel good about the exchanges and will want to connect with him or her more."

"If the person who offered the help tries to own the gratitude of their dependents, he or she will end up exploiting those very people who came for protection and guidance."

"When a person in a superior position begins to manipulate, control, or sexually exploit those dependents, it is just like incest. When people come for help, they leave themselves open. They view the authority as a big brother or parent. If the caretaker then exploits those people who have become vulnerable, heavy karmic consequences will follow."

---

## Slide 8: Purity as the Major Force

"As devotees, we want to have the intensity and the purity that invokes divine intervention. Then, due to the devotion, other things will happen. Shortages in Krishna consciousness are maya."

"Purity is the major force. It has so much to do with simplicity, austerities, perseverance, and strong faith. As Albert Einstein once said, 'You cannot solve a problem with the same mindset that created it.' If there is a problem, we have to take a step back and be bold enough to look at ourselves first."

## Slide 9A: The Living Example — Śrī Jayānanda Thākur (Part 1)
### **Avoiding Praise Like the Plague**

> **Running from Fame & The "Most Fallen" Game:**  
> "Humility was certainly Jayānanda's most prominent quality. He treated everyone as his superior, even new devotees. Although his service was glorious, he never wanted any glory. **He avoided praise like the plague.**
> 
> Devotees were always playing this funny game: we would praise him, and he would tell us how fallen he was. Praise naturally would spring to our lips, but we could not praise him, or he would just walk away."

> **Renunciation Without the Trappings:**  
> "He was truly in the renounced order, without all the trappings. He didn't want the attention. He didn't want people to bow down when he entered a room, nor the praise of a sannyāsī. Jayānanda was too busy being our father and mother and doing all the needful things."

*Sources: Remembering Jayananda by Kalakantha Dasa & Jayananda Thakur ACBSP by Vishoka Dasa*

---

## Slide 9B: The Living Example — Śrī Jayānanda Thākur (Part 2)
### **The Pūjārī of Guru's Grace (100% Credit to Prabhupāda)**

> **The Analogy of the Pūjārī (Servant of Servants):**  
> "Just as a good pūjārī performs his service on the altar in such a way that he doesn’t draw any attention to himself but allows the full focus to remain on the Deities, in the same way Jayānanda does the needful without any flair and attention to his own personal needs. He preferred to work under other authorities, so that he could truly be the servant of the servants."

> **Endowed Only by Guru's Grace:**  
> "An advanced devotee never claims to have his own strength, but always knows that it is only by the guru's grace that he has any shakti at all. This was totally Jayānanda, through and through. **He never took credit for anything; he always gave all the credit to Śrīla Prabhupāda.**
> 
> He spoke more about Prabhupāda than Kṛṣṇa. Never for a moment did he think that his good fortune was due to any effort of his own."

*Sources: Radha-Damodara Vilasa by Vaiyasaki Dasa & Jayananda Thakur ACBSP by Vishoka Dasa*
