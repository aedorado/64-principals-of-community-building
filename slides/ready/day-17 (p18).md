## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem
### **Sentimental Religion & The Defending Ego**

* **The Insanity of Denial:**  
  "Being in the material world means that we are deviant. The fact that we have material bodies indicates that we are running away from Krishna. We have been hiding for so many lifetimes and finding so many reasons to remain in this insane state."

* **The Trap of Sentimental "Blessings":**  
  "Many people have the idea that the spiritual master is supposed to look very holy, perform some bhajana, and bless everyone before leaving. If you are sick but the doctor tells you that you are healthy, you will not get the necessary medicine and treatment."

* **The Blindness of False Ego:**  
  "We have the tendency to defend ourselves when another person starts speaking honestly. This is the nature of the false ego. Our perception of ourselves is not always what others see. We tend to have a completely different image of ourselves."

---

## Slide 3: Principle 18 — Truth Over Flattery
### **The Scalpel vs. Blowing Air**

Principle 18: "A flatterer can never become a guru or a preacher."

"Such people can never become gurus because a guru is supposed to be heavy. The guru’s main duty is to chastise the disciples."

"It is the guru’s duty to chastise us, and between chastisements, he gives us some encouragement to prepare us for the next chastisement."

---

## Slide 4: Love Behind Heavy Words
### **Stopping Spiritual Suicide**

"Srila Prabhupada often used very heavy words to describe the deviants, which would offend some people. Some people used to get angry with Srila Prabhupada when they heard him speaking so strongly. They would wonder how such interactions were spiritual."

"I once met a group of Mayavadis who approached me, asking, ‘Why is your Guruji always attacking us? Why does he have to attack?’ I told them, ‘It is because he loves you. He is trying to help you see that, by thinking that the Supreme Personality of Godhead does not have relationships, does not have a body, or does not give and receive love, you are an offender. He wants to help you stop committing spiritual suicide.’"

## Slide 6: Peer Feedback & Writing Down Criticism
### **Bypassing the Ego with Pen and Paper**

"We can ask our associates who have served with us over a period of years for advice and feedback. Who knows us better than somebody who has served with us for five, ten, or fifteen years? Even if we don’t feel that they have an accurate perception of us, we have a responsibility to act in a way that minimizes their disturbance since we are serving together."

"When you do ask another person for feedback or criticism, take a piece of paper and write down what they say. Then reflect on their feedback later. It is helpful to write it down because we have the tendency to defend ourselves when another person starts speaking honestly."

"As a brahmacari, I used to ask for feedback all the time. I would literally write down the devotees’ comments: *‘You chant too loud. You are always reading while everybody is chanting. You are puffed up... You should not be artificially austere.’* After hearing, I would often try to adjust my services in a way that would bring about the greatest benefit to the whole group."

## Slide 7: Beyond Human Calculation

"You may even have a spiritual mentor who may not be the best manager—most of us are not—but if you follow the instruction, then you will make spiritual progress. It is really not just about managing the material energy anyway. The most important things that happen in our devotional life and in our movement happen as a result of purity."

"When successes happen based on manpower, material expertise and money, it is not necessarily very spiritual. It might be a nice administrative arrangement, but spiritual things happen beyond our normal capacity—beyond our finances, material expertise and manpower."

"Shortages in Krishna consciousness are maya. Krishna owns everything. It is not difficult for Him to make a few arrangements if we have enough purity... When we lose some of our pioneering spirit, we end up simply maintaining, which then leads to stagnation. Purity is the major force."

"If there is a problem, we have to take a step back and be bold enough to look at ourselves first."

---

## Slide 8: The Living Example

"We notice that the movement has grown due to the sincerity of a few people. A few devotees went out on sankirtana, but because they were serious, people came and bought books. As they continued in their endeavors, people joined and others donated facilities. Somehow temples were gradually established all over the world. As devotees, we want to have the intensity and the purity that invokes divine intervention."

"Krishna wants us to come back faster than we want to return, and He wants us to help others return home as well. However, we should also know that Krishna is called akincana—sometimes He gives special favor to a devotee by taking everything away."

"Practically every project started meagerly. And as long as there is purity, it will continue to grow and grow. It starts with a little sincerity and determination... Purity is the major force. It has so much to do with simplicity, austerities, perseverance, and strong faith."

## Slide 10: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **The Surgical Knife vs. Flattery:** Why is our false ego so naturally allergic to straightforward correction? When a mentor or peer points out a deficiency in our behavior or attitude, do we reflexively justify ourselves, or do we welcome the surgical cut that cures our deviance?

2. **The Humility to Invite Feedback:** Bhakti Tirtha Swami literally carried a pen and paper to write down his peers' blunt criticisms so his mind wouldn't defend itself in the moment. In our sangha, do we possess enough trust and maturity to ask those who serve alongside us: *"What blind spots do you see in my devotional life?"*
