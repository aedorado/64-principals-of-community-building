## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem
### **Fair-Weather Faith, Shallow Preparation & The Prison Mindset**

* **Phobias & Fair-Weather Faith:**  
  "Most of us hated exams in school. Some people experience all types of phobias and psychological disturbances when they have to sit in an exam hall. It is comparable to the execution room. Some people believe in God when their lives are unfolding according to their own plans, but when they face challenges, they lose their faith."

* **Cracking Under Pressure:**  
  "When some people in school come to take their exam, they only partially know the material and consequently make many mistakes due to the pressure. Since they have not really imbibed the material into their consciousness, they crack under the pressure."

* **The Hellish Prison Duality:**  
  "In these realms, people are always experiencing duplicity, diplomacy, and the duality of friends and enemies. Individuals experience constant phobias and fears as they try to see what they can get for themselves, or how to abuse and misuse someone else before that person abuses them."

## Slide 3: Principle 29 — The Examination Hall
### **Seeing Kṛṣṇa in the Background & Preparing for the Spiritual World**

Principle 29: "This material nature, which is averse to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is an examination hall."

"Srila Bhaktisiddhanta describes the material world as an examination hall—a place in which people come to take tests... In other cases, a person has imbibed the message so deeply that the pressure does not affect him or her. The student answers the questions just as smoothly as he or she did while studying."

"It is a big test of our faith to recognize that Krishna is there in the background... When we come to a point at which we can remember and lovingly glorify the Lord regardless of the circumstances, then we are already becoming rightful residents of the spiritual world. By not latching onto the patterns of the material world or prison, we are preparing ourselves for the spiritual world."

---

## Slide 4: Practice for Eternity — The Musician’s Equilibrium
### **Private Diligence Determines Public Stability**

"We can see our daily activities as practice for what we can ultimately do eternally. The quality of our practice will determine our real position."

"For instance, the quality of a musician’s practice will show when the individual has to perform. Although the average person usually feels nervous during a performance, if they have taken their practice seriously, they will be able to excel despite the nervousness. On the other hand, if the musician did not practice diligently and attentively, the individual will lose his or her equilibrium when placed before an audience. The musician will lose stability, consequently performing poorly."

---

## Slide 5: The Final Exam & Transcending Duality
### **Preparing Consciousness for Death**

"As devotees, we understand that the biggest test is death. At that time, our consciousness will depend on our activities throughout our lives and the priorities we have held, which will determine our next destination. If, throughout our lives, we constantly feel confusion, bewilderment, anger, or faithlessness, how will we have great faith when we have to confront the biggest test—death—in which the mind, the intelligence, and the senses fail us in so many ways?"

"Conversely, if a person has consistently acted on the platform of transcendence on a daily basis without playing into the normative schemes, and consistently glorified the Lord in every situation, then naturally the transition will not be difficult. When we are able to transcend the duality in spite of the circumstances, it indicates that we are becoming highly spiritual."

## Slide 6A: Living Example (Principle 29) — Bhagavān Dāsa in the Mughal Court
### **Refusing to Crack Under Life-or-Death Pressure**

> **The Imperial Ban on *Tilaka* and *Mālā*:**  
> When the Mughal emperor noticed many subjects in Mathurā wearing *tilaka* and *mālā*—considering it a refutation of his authority—he issued a strict proclamation forbidding anyone from wearing them. Out of fear of losing their lives, many Vaiṣṇavas abandoned their sacred markings under the pressure of the test.  
> 
> **Standing Fearlessly in the Exam Hall:**  
> *“One devotee, Bhagavan Das [pupil of Yuta Khojiji and Shyamdas, as related by Priyadāsa], was so filled with the blissful ocean of bhakti that he dared to present himself before the emperor wearing his tilaka and mālā. The emperor was so impressed and pleased by this display of faith and courage that he permitted Bhagavan Das to continue to reside in Mathura. It is also said that the saint subsequently founded a temple dedicated to Harideva.”*

*Source: A.W. Entwistle, Vaiṣṇava Tilakas (pp. 40–41, London, 1982)*

---

## Slide 6B: Living Example (Principle 29) — Bihārīnādeva & Emperor Akbar
### **Passing the Emperor’s *Darbār* Test with Extra *Mālās* & *Tilaka***

> **Akbar Summons the Vaiṣṇavas to Test Their Faith:**  
> Seeking to test the steadfastness of the Vaiṣṇavas, Emperor Akbar summoned all the saints and *mahantas* to his *darbār*, explicitly warning that **none must appear wearing *tilaka* or *mālā***.  
> 
> **Extra *Tulasī-Mālās* & Elongated *Tilaka*:**  
> Among all assembled saints, **Bihārīnādeva** (a follower of Swāmī Haridāsa) alone resolved to wear the sacred emblems of his faith—not from egotism, for Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī had appeared to him in a dream telling him not to be afraid.  
> *“Accordingly, Biharinadev put on extra tulasī-mālās and enhanced his tilaka by smearing it with his thumb so that it extended down to the tip of his nose... At the darbār, Akbar proclaimed that Biharinadev was foremost among the Vaiṣṇavas on account of his steadfastness.”*

*Source: A.W. Entwistle, Vaiṣṇava Tilakas (pp. 40–41, London, 1982)*
