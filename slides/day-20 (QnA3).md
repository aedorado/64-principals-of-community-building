## Slide 2: Question 7
### **Palatability vs. Compromising the Truth**

“Question: Aren’t we guilty of minimizing the truth if we don’t give the full message of Krishna consciousness in every possible situation?”

---

## Slide 3: Answer 7 — Part I
### **Planting Seeds vs. Intimidation**

“There is a difference between minimizing the truth and planting seeds that set a foundation for people to later receive more. We minimize the truth when we have an opportunity to speak, but we refrain from doing so due to intimidation. Devotees are to be communicators, meaning that we must understand the field of activities. There were times when Srila Prabhupada spoke directly, and there were also times when he did not preach at all, but simply treated a person kindly. For instance, Srila Prabhupada acted kindly with George Harrison’s gardener who then remembered him fondly. Srila Prabhupada did not preach any philosophy at all; he was just kind to him.”

---

## Slide 4: Answer 7 — Part II
### ** Universal Relevance vs. Sectarianism**

“I write many of my own books in a similar way. I address cutting edge topics while also offering the universal wisdom of Vedic knowledge. We want them to recognize that we are not just a sectarian group of religionists absorbed in some weird philosophy; rather, we are people who have a higher understanding about life and society. Once people see us as relevant, that our message deals with practical issues and can improve the quality of life, then our differences become like a novelty. Preaching is powerful, and our message is very strong, but it has to connect.”

---

## Slide 5: Answer 7 — Part III
### **Vision With Love**

“We are spiritual revolutionists who are to have compassion and vision. When people have vision without love, they will be motivated for the wrong reasons. This is true in our own temples as well. Conversely, when people have love without vision, nothing gets accomplished. There has to be vision with love so that devotees become motivated and enthused. Otherwise, the devotees in our temples will lose enthusiasm and want to leave due to boredom and dissatisfaction.”

---

## Slide 6: Answer 7 — Part IV
### **Propensity-Based Care**

“Our duty is to know the people with whom we are associating and understand how to engage them according to their propensities. A leader should know his constituents as a mother knows her children, and he should care for them to the same extent. We cannot always give everyone what they want in terms of service, but the closer we come to engaging people according to their propensities, the better. On the other hand, if a devotee with brahminical qualities, for example, is doing mainly vaisya work, collecting money day after day, they will eventually lose enthusiasm.”

---

## Slide 7: Answer 7 — Part V
### **Care and Encouragement of Volunteers**

“As devotees, we should consider these factors even more than managers and CEOs because our devotees are volunteering their services. It is different when people work for money because even if they hate their job and boss, they tolerate it since they receive a paycheck. When people are volunteering, on the other hand, it has much more to do with enthusing them and letting them know how much the leaders care for them. Then, even when they feel a little distraught, they will think, ‘My leader cares for me so much that I don’t want to disappoint him or her.’”

---

## Slide 8: Question 8
### **Over-Endeavoring and Failed Plans**

“Question: What if we have a strong desire to serve Krishna by working on a specific project, but no matter how hard we try, it just does not work out?”

---

## Slide 9: Answer 8
### **The Prayer of the Servant's Plans**

“It could indicate that we are over-endeavoring for a material result rather than a spiritual goal. When we act according to our own ideas, we can try to see it as service, but if it does not work after repeated endeavors, it could be a sign that we are not taking the healthiest approach. As we involve ourselves in different projects, we should be in a prayerful state: ‘Krishna, if my plans do not follow Your desire, then don’t let them manifest. Please put me in a situation that helps me to do the right thing in the right way.’ If we make this prayer, Krishna will reciprocate. On the other hand, if we are constantly trying to act according to our own intelligence, we will only meet with frustration.”

---

## Slide 10: Question 9
### **The Science of Self-Love**

“Question: How do we love ourselves?”

---

## Slide 11: Answer 9 — Part I
### **Loving our Real Self**

“What is the real self? The self is not just an acculturated personality, nor is it our false ego or our senses. Loving our real self involves acting in ways that will accelerate our ability to go back to Godhead faster. When we love ourselves, we do those things that increase our love of Krishna and decrease our desire for sense gratification. When we address those aspects of ourselves, we are really loving the self. The love will then bring great results and give us the ability to help and love others in a more genuine way. We cannot love others if we constantly commit violence to ourselves. The first love we must consummate is with our self.”

---

## Slide 12: Answer 9 — Part II
### **Material Addiction is Soul Violence**

“A person who constantly degrades himself cannot deeply love anybody else. If somebody constantly smokes, eats flesh, breaks the principles, or even has a large ego, these self-destructive behaviors will prevent him from loving others deeply. How can people who are not carriers of love distribute it to others? However, if we love people who do not love themselves, we can feel sad to watch them destroy themselves. We can genuinely try to love them, but they cannot love us deeply because they have circumvented it by their own self-destructiveness. We have to find out what the self really is.”

---

## Slide 13: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection (Questions 7, 8, 9)**

1. Preaching Strategy: Bhakti Tirtha Swami advises us to plant seeds and avoid intimidating the public with sectarian dogmas. How can we ensure our local outreach deals with relevant, practical issues of life rather than presenting Vaishnavism as an exotic, weird cult?

2. The Real Science of Self-Love: B.T. Swami defines self-love as doing things that accelerate our return to Godhead, stating that we cannot love others if we commit violence to our own souls through self-destructive material habits. How does this definition challenge the modern commercialized concepts of "self-care" or "self-indulgence"?

## Slide 14: Hrdaya Seva
### **An Immediate Heart Offering**

**The Silent Surrender of Personal Projects (60 Seconds):**
Close your eyes and sit in absolute stillness. Bring to your mind a personal plan, desire, or service project you are desperately trying to force through, despite repeated obstacles or failures. Acknowledge the false ego and material ambition behind it. Mentally lay that project down entirely on Krishna's altar.

**The Prayer of the Beggar:**
Pray silently: “My dear Lord, if my plans do not match Your will, please block them completely. I do not trust my own intelligence. Please place me in any situation, service, or asrama that pleases Your heart, and grant me the humility to find my absolute satisfaction in simply being Your puppet.”
