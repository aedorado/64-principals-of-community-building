## Slide 2: The Root of the Problem (Principle 23)
### **Mechanical Ritualism Without Personal Practice**

* **Speaking Without Living the Message:**  
  "Speaking the philosophy without honestly being an example of the message falls into the category of karma-kanda according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta."

* **The Illusion of Mechanical Teaching:**  
  "Transcendental knowledge cannot be taught or accepted mechanically."

---

## Slide 3: Principle 23 — Practice Is Real Preaching
### **Uniting *Vacana*, *Pracāra* & *Sadācāra***

Principle 23: "Preaching without practicing is nothing but a ritual of karma-kanda."

"Real preaching is through example. A proper devotee’s *vacana* (speech) should be in line with his *pracara* (behavior), and his *sadacara* (good behavior) ultimately is his real preaching. By engaging one’s senses in devotional activities, one begins to acquire causeless knowledge:  
*vāsudeve bhagavati bhakti-yogaḥ prayojitaḥ*  
*janayaty āśu vairāgyaṁ jñānaṁ ca yad ahaitukam*  
*(By rendering devotional service unto the Personality of Godhead, Sri Krishna, one immediately acquires causeless knowledge and detachment from the world.)* — SB 1.2.7"

"The authentic devotee is not interested in superficialities or externalities, but is focused on the essence. Therefore, his preaching includes practice as he shows by example what is to be done and how it is to be done. This is a sign of not only his faith and genuineness, but is also a sign of his compassion for others."

## Slide 4: Living Example (Principle 23) — The Blue Jackal vs. Genuine Ācārya
### **External Titles vs. Authentic *Sadācāra* (Śrī Śrīmad Gour Govinda Swami)**

> **The Blue Jackal Parable (*Preaching Without Inner Nature*):**  
> *“Once a jackal fell into the blue dye-water of a washerman. The jackal became blue. Then he went to the forest and declared to everyone, ‘I am the king of the animals.’ All of the animals said, ‘Oh yes! He is strange looking. He might be king.’ Thus they made the jackal king and he sat on the throne.*  
> 
> *Then when evening came, all of the jackals began howling, ‘Youka hou! Youka hou!’ So the jackal king also started howling, and then everyone saw, ‘Oh! He’s a jackal! Beat him! He’s not king!’ In this way, he was revealed.”*  
> 
> **Genuine Simplicity vs. Blowing One’s Own Trumpet:**  
> *“This world is filled with hypocrites... He may come and beat his own drum, blow his own trumpet, and make some politics... Although he was a householder, Srila Prabhupada got the full mercy of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. He never spent any time in the maṭha, but he became ācārya because he was very, very simple, not envious. You cannot put a stamp on someone, ‘You are ācārya.’ Or else the day may come to prove that he’s not an ācārya... Krishna and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu will reveal everything.”*

*Source: Q&A with Śrī Śrīmad Gour Govinda Swami Mahārāja (Paris, 1987)*

## Slide 5: The Root of the Problem (Principle 24)
### **The Proprietary *Gṛhamedhī* Household**

* **Acting as Proprietor of One's Territory:**  
  "When someone functions as a grhamedhi, he or she is so attached to the whole idea of household life and all that comes as a byproduct. Such householders view everything as their territory and they act as proprietors of all they survey."

* **Mundane Struggle for Survival:**  
  "When householders center their lives around themselves and their individual needs, it creates a mundane atmosphere of just struggling for survival and sense gratification."

---

## Slide 6: Principle 24 — The Alchemy of Household Service
### **Transforming Greed and Lust into Compassion and Love**

Principle 24: "If one just serves the Supreme Personality of Godhead and His devotees, his attachment to his household will diminish."

"If a householder can channel some of that energy into serving the devotees and the Supreme Lord, then the person will not have this grhamedhi consciousness."

"When they use what they have in Krishna’s service, it becomes devotional service, which is beneficial for everyone. Devotional service transforms greed and lust into selflessness, compassion, and love."

## Slide 7A: Living Example (Principle 24) — Trilocana & His Wife
### **A Household Dedicated to Serving Saintly Persons**

> **1. Trilocana’s Desire & The Lord Arriving in Disguise:**  
> Though appearing in a *vaiśya* family, Śrī Trilocana served the devotees with only his wife to assist him, longing for a servant who understood the moods of saintly persons. One day, the Supreme Lord Himself arrived at his door wearing only a torn blanket and broken shoes:  
> *“Trilochan asked, ‘Will you work for me? I need a servant to serve saintly persons.’ The Lord replied, ‘Every day I eat five or six kilos of rice. Because of this, people become upset with me and will not hire me... As far as serving the devotees is concerned, I have been doing that for my whole life... My name is Antaryami. I agree to become your servant.’”*  
> 
> **2. Welcoming Antaryāmī into the Home:**  
> *“Sri Trilochan said, ‘You can eat as much as you want. Do not feel shy about eating.’ Saying this, Sri Trilochan gave Antaryami a pair of new shoes and a new blanket, and bathed him very thoroughly. Trilocana then said to his wife, ‘Keep this Antaryami as your servant and do not hesitate to feed him to his full satisfaction... **He will render service to the devotees every day, and you should serve him every day. It is our duty to live together throughout our lives, serving the devotees and singing the glories of Lord Hari.**’”*

*Source: **Śrī Nābhā Dāsa jī kṛta Bhakta-māla** with the commentary of **Priyadāsa** (1766 A.D.)*

## Slide 7B: Living Example (Principle 24) — Gadai Giri
### **Sacrificing Business Attachment for *Hari-Bhajana* and Devotees**

> **From Traveling Merchant to Village Sanctuary:**  
> *“Gadai Giri was a businessman who regularly came to the area to sell utensils and bell-metal items... Gadai Giri was a gentle and saintly person. He would rise early each morning and perform kīrtan. Then after taking some flat rice and water he would go out selling brass utensils door to door. When evening arrived he would spend the night in some village and join with the local people in their daily custom of singing kīrtan and reciting Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.”*  
> 
> **Centering an Entire Community Around Gopāl:**  
> *“Appreciating Gadai Giri’s saintly qualities, the local zamindar offered to give him some nearby land... He cleared a small section of the jungle and built a house and a small temple wherein he established a deity of Dadhi Baman (Lord Jagannath)... **Gadai Giri left his brass business and simply engaged himself in bhajan and kīrtan. Many wandering sādhus and sannyāsis came to join with Gadai Giri’s kīrtan.** Gradually other people came to stay permanently, and a village developed in that place in Gadai Giri’s name.”*

*Source: **Gopal Jiu: The Beloved Deity of Srila Gour Govinda Swami**, published by Gopal Jiu Publications, Bhubaneswar, 1999*

## Slide 7C: Living Example (Principle 24) — Lalacharya
### **Welcoming Vaikuṇṭha Associates into the Household Courtyard**

> **Humble Hospitality Amidst Local Pride:**  
> When a group of the Lord’s associates, assuming the form of *brāhmaṇas*, arrived at Lalacharya’s house, local envious *brāhmaṇas* waited outside to ridicule them. *“When Lalacharya saw the Lord’s associates, he fell flat onto the ground to offer his obeisances. He then prayed, ‘O lords! You have shown your kindness upon me by considering me as your servant. Now please sit down and honor prasādam.’”*  
> 
> **The Lord’s Associates Depart to Vaikuṇṭha:**  
> *“After taking their meal, the associates of the Lord departed. From the courtyard they flew into the air on their way to Vaikuntha. Meanwhile, the local Brahmins who were waiting saw the Brahmin invitees leave through the sky and so, realizing that they were divine personalities, they felt sorry. Indeed, their pride was smashed... Having now understood the glories of Lord’s prasāda, they carefully picked up the particles of prasāda that were scattered on the ground and honored them.”*  
> 
> *“Thereafter, the king of devotees, Sri Lalacharya, told them, ‘It is because of your mercy that I was blessed with the association of the Lord’s associates from Vaikuntha. Today I had the darśaṇa of the four-armed associates of the Lord that are described in the Purāṇas.’”*

*Source: **Śrī Nābhā Dāsa jī kṛta Bhakta-māla** with the commentary of **Priyadāsa** (1766 A.D.)*

## Slide 8: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection**

1. **Speech (*Vacana*) vs. Conduct (*Sadācāra*):** Principle 23 warns that preaching without practicing reduces spiritual life to mere *karma-kāṇḍa*, while genuine compassion shows others *how* to live by example. Where in our life do we catch ourselves speaking high philosophy that we are not yet living quietly in our own behavior?

2. **From Proprietor to Caretaker (*Gṛhamedhī* vs. *Gṛhastha*):** Bhakti Tirtha Swami describes *gṛhamedhī* consciousness as treating our home, wealth, and family as "our territory." What is one practical shift in our home routine or hospitality that would turn our dwelling into a sanctuary for serving Kṛṣṇa and the devotees?
