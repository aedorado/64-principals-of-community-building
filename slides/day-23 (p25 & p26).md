## Slide 2: Principle 25 — The Disease of Collection
### **Shedding as the Impetus for Progress**

> “Our main disease is to collect objects that are not related to Krishna.”

“Our main disease is to collect objects that are not related to Krishna. Spiritual elevation is not so much about acquiring but about shedding. We want to shed anything that hinders our progress on the path of devotional service. One of the principles that leads to falldown is excessive collecting.”

---

## Slide 3: Accumulation vs. Elimination
### **Moving Back from the Superfluous**

“By investing our valuable time, energy, and intelligence in unnecessary and unwanted things, we create our own obstacles. We have this disease of accumulation, but the process of bhakti involves elimination. By backing away from superfluous things, we will be able to move towards the Divine Couple.”

“One’s devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) overendeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain; (3) talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters; (4) practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them and not for the sake of spiritual advancement, or rejecting the rules and regulations of the scriptures and working independently or whimsically; (5) associating with worldly-minded persons who are not interested in Krishna consciousness; and (6) being greedy for mundane achievements.”

---

## Slide 4: The Distraction of Commodities
### **Forgetting Our Primary Spiritual Needs**

“When we are too involved in collecting things that are not relevant to our genuine spiritual needs, it distracts us from focusing on our primary needs and spiritual growth. The more we pursue temporary concerns and commodities, the more we become forgetful and attached to the illusory energies.”

---

## Slide 5: Favorable and Unfavorable Acceptance
### **Vigilance and Natural Alignment**

“Maya tries very hard to keep the living entities distracted from pursuing sanatana-dharma. As we keep our lives focused on Krishna, we will naturally accept things favorable for devotional service and reject unfavorable things.”

---

## Slide 6: Principle 26 — Peons of Mercy
### **Moving Past Material Artisanship**

> “We have not come to this world to become an artisan of wood or stone; we are only peons of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s mercy.”

“We have not come to this world to become an artisan of wood or stone; we are only peons of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s mercy. Krishna does not need anything; He is self-satisfied. Nevertheless, we cook for Him, bathe Him, dress Him, build beautiful buildings for Him, and distribute books and prasadam as a way to preach about Him.”

---

## Slide 7: What Krishna Really Wants
### **Shedding the Cheating Propensity**

“But what does He really want? He ultimately wants us to give our hearts and give up sense gratification. Due to our cheating propensity, we are willing to give so many things to the Lord as long as we do not have to give our hearts. We try to ingratiate Krishna through our offerings, but it is not the same as giving our selves.”

---

## Slide 8: The Definition of a Peon
### **Understanding Compulsory Servitude**

“Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that we are to be peons of the Lord’s mercy. A peon is one who is held in compulsory servitude to a master for the working out of a debt. He is trying to help us understand our position in relation to the Lord.”

---

## Slide 9: Indebted to Lord Caitanya
### **Servitude Cloaked in Causeless Mercy**

“Lord Caitanya has given us so much mercy by teaching us in the mood of a devotee how to serve Krishna. We are seriously indebted to Him and we are so blessed that He extends His mercy so profusely. We can never fully repay those special souls who connect us with Lord Caitanya’s mercy, but we can reflect constantly with gratitude, appreciation, and excitement.”

---

## Slide 10: Vaishnava Sangha
### **Heart-to-Heart Reflection (Integration Bridge)**

1. The Culture of Shedding: Bhakti Tirtha Swami explains that spiritual elevation is about shedding (elimination) rather than acquisition, and that over-collecting (atyahara) spoils our bhakti. What are some specific, unnecessary material objects, digital distractions, or prestigious commitments we have collected that we need to actively "shed" to simplify our devotional lives?

2. Giving the Heart: We are warned of our cheating propensity, where we are willing to give Krishna beautiful physical offerings (like wood, stone, money, or labor) as long as we do not have to give Him our actual hearts. How can we transition from mechanical, transactional service to genuinely offering our innermost desires, vulnerabilities, and love to the Lord and His devotees?

---

## Slide 11: Hrdaya Seva
### **An Immediate Heart Offering**

**The Silent Search for Shedding (60 Seconds):**
Close your eyes and sit in quiet stillness. Bring to your mind one specific material possession, comfort, or prideful attachment you have collected that is not relevant to your spiritual needs. Realize how it distracts you from your relationship with Krishna. Mentally offer this attachment to Lord Caitanya, begging Him to cleanse your heart of the desire for accumulation.

**The Action Vow of Simplification:**
Commit to identifying three unnecessary items in your physical space today and safely donating, selling, or discarding them as a practical exercise in shedding. Mentally pray: “My dear Lord, let me not act as an artisan of wood and stone, trying to cheat You with external shows. Let me become a sincere, grateful peon of Your mercy, offering You my entire heart without holding anything back.”
